# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-13

Thursday’s digest draws entirely from the preceding 24–48 hours. Two peer-reviewed studies probe high-temperature superconductivity at complementary limits: a single CuO₂ plane and a pressure-tuned nickelate above 100 K. NOvA supplies a cleaner antineutrino interaction benchmark. In mathematics, new preprints sharply narrow the Grothendieck constant and claim a proof of Gluck’s 1985 conjecture; both remain unreviewed.

## Top discoveries

### 1. A cuprate remains superconducting with only one CuO₂ plane
Luo, Song, Yu and collaborators isolate a monolayer of Bi₂Sr₂CuO₆₊δ containing a single copper-oxide plane. It remains superconducting, with an optimal transition temperature only about 10% below that of thicker material. Precise oxygen control tunes the monolayer from an insulator through an anomalous metal to a superconductor as temperature approaches zero; scaling near the transition yields a divergent critical exponent.

**Why it matters:** The experiment establishes a minimal and highly tunable cuprate that retains most of the high-temperature superconducting scale, making it possible to separate genuinely two-dimensional physics from interlayer effects.

### 2. Nickelate superconducting signatures reach 100.5 K under pressure
Qiu, Chen, Semenok and collaborators synthesized La₃₋ₓNdₓNi₂O₇ with unusually broad neodymium substitution and measured it at high pressure. For the x = 2.1 composition, electrical resistance begins dropping near 93 K and radio-frequency transmission shows a superconducting signature at 100.5 K. The substitutions also change the lattice compression, spin-density-wave order and pressure required for a structural transition, giving clues about which ingredients track the higher transition temperature. This is not ambient-pressure superconductivity.

**Why it matters:** Reaching the 100 K scale extends the frontier for bilayer nickelates and supplies structural and magnetic design clues for a still-new family of high-temperature superconductors.

### 3. NOvA measures antineutrino–hydrogen scattering with record statistics
The NOvA Collaboration selected 35,509 interactions in which a muon antineutrino scatters from a free proton in hydrogen and produces a positive muon and neutron. At an average energy of 1.9 GeV, it reports a total cross section of \(0.538\times10^{-38}\,\mathrm{cm}^2\), with statistical and non-flux systematic errors much smaller than the remaining beam-flux uncertainty. The sample is the highest-statistics (anti)neutrino–hydrogen data set reported so far.

**Why it matters:** Free hydrogen avoids the nuclear effects that blur measurements in heavier targets. It provides a cleaner reference for calibrating antineutrino beams and modeling the interactions used by long-baseline oscillation experiments.

### 4. New bounds fix the tenths digit of the Grothendieck constant
Saha, Li, Xue, Chaudhuri, Klivans, Kothari and Meka prove \(6\pi/11\le K_G\le\pi/[2\log(1+\sqrt2)]-3.47\times10^{-4}\). Their lower bound limits asymptotically optimal Krivine rounding schemes, while the upper bound constructs a new high-dimensional asymptotic family. The bounds determine the previously unknown tenths digit of the real constant as 7.

**Why it matters:** The Grothendieck constant links Banach-space geometry, semidefinite-program rounding, approximation algorithms and quantum information. The preprint substantially narrows a central quantity whose exact value remains unknown.

### 5. Gluck’s 1985 conjecture for finite solvable groups is proved
Zhang proves that every finite solvable group \(G\) obeys \(|G:\mathbf F(G)|\le b(G)^2\), where \(\mathbf F(G)\) is its largest normal nilpotent subgroup and \(b(G)\) is its largest irreducible complex-character degree. The argument develops new orbit bounds that avoid counterexamples to the more direct large-orbit strategy.

**Why it matters:** If verified, the preprint closes a four-decade problem connecting a finite group’s internal structure with its representation theory.

## Watchlist

- **Ultracold-neutron limits on a sterile sector (arXiv:2608.12173)** — No field-dependent neutron loss was found; the resulting mirror-neutron exclusions beat a model-dependent neutron-star estimate in part of the scanned range.
- **Leech-lattice auxiliary function (arXiv:2608.12094)** — Zhang and Yang construct the exact function conjectured by Cohn and Kumar and recover the nontrivial Leech-lattice shell coefficients.
- **Length-four index conjecture (arXiv:2608.12310)** — Li, Yuan, Yuan and Zhang remove the remaining modulus-size restriction for minimal zero-sum sequences over cyclic groups.
- **Near-optimal additive energy on convex curves (arXiv:2608.12316)** — Cushman, Demeter and Wu reach the natural near-cubic bound under only strict convexity and derive stronger sumset bounds for convex sequences; the preprint merits specialist verification.
- **Alignment-free hBN magnetometry (Communications Materials, 12 Aug)** — Randomly oriented boron-vacancy spins in commercial polycrystalline hBN enable room-temperature scalar sensing without precise alignment, but present sensitivity is roughly two orders of magnitude below a plasmonically enhanced single-crystal implementation.
- **Quantum-limited diffractive imaging (arXiv:2608.12300)** — A proposed optical neural network with photon counting saturates the calculated precision limit for separable measurements in simulations; physical implementation remains to be shown.
- **Abiotic soil solar-energy conversion (Nature Communications, 12 Aug)** — Goethite–kaolinite interfaces produce four times the photocurrent of pure goethite, while the estimated ecosystem contributions are process-specific theoretical upper bounds.
- **Weighted Hilbert inequality (arXiv:2608.12315)** — Rodgers gives an explicit limiting counterexample showing that the Montgomery–Vaughan optimal constant is strictly greater than \(\pi\).
