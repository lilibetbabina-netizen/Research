# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-13

Thursday’s notes centre on 12 Aug VoRs: Nature reports a superconducting Bi-2201 monolayer with one CuO₂ plane, Nature Communications pushes bilayer nickelate superconducting signatures near 100 K via Nd substitution, Nature Physics gives end-to-end thermal and ground-state preparation with a single ancilla, conductivity data imply a nearly dry lower-mantle bridgmanite, and a 12 Aug preprint settles Gluck’s conjecture for finite solvable groups.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Superconducting monolayer cuprate with a single CuO₂ plane
Luo, Song, Yu, Ma and collaborators (Nature, 12 Aug) isolate monolayer Bi₂Sr₂CuO₆₊δ (Bi-2201) — a single CuO₂ plane — and find superconductivity survives with only about a 10% drop in optimal transition temperature. Controlled oxygenation extends the doping map, revealing an anomalous metal between insulator and superconductor at low temperature and anomalous scaling with a divergent critical exponent at the superconductor–insulator quantum phase transition.

**Why it matters:** Cuprate physics reaches the true single-plane, van der Waals limit, testing which high-Tc ingredients need only one CuO₂ plane and exposing quantum-critical behaviour between the Mott insulator and the superconductor.

### 2. High-temperature superconductivity near 100 K in Nd-substituted La₃Ni₂O₇
Qiu, Chen, Semenok, Zhong and co-authors (Nature Communications, 12 Aug) synthesise La₃₋ₓNdₓNi₂O₇ with record Nd substitution. Chemical pressure strengthens the spin-density wave and shifts the structural transition; under high pressure, transport shows superconductivity at all doping levels, with onset Tc ~93 K and RF-transmission signatures up to 100.5 K for x = 2.1 near 33 GPa.

**Why it matters:** Bilayer nickelates are a young high-Tc platform; pushing reported signatures to the 100 K scale and linking them to magnetism and structure clarifies how to raise Tc further.

### 3. End-to-end quantum thermal and ground-state preparation with one ancilla
Ding, Zhan, Preskill and Lin (Nature Physics, 12 Aug) prepare thermal and ground states by forward evolution under a system–bath Hamiltonian whose bath is a single reusable ancilla qubit. They prove the fixed point approximates the target and give mixing-time guarantees for a class of physically relevant Hamiltonians suited to early fault-tolerant devices.

**Why it matters:** State preparation dominates the cost of many quantum-simulation algorithms; ancilla-light methods with efficiency proofs cut a central overhead.

### 4. Nearly dry bridgmanite in Earth’s lower mantle
Okuda, Ohta, Hirose and colleagues (Nature Communications, 12 Aug) measure conductivity of hydrous (Fe,Al)-bearing bridgmanite to 47 GPa and 2210 K. Water boosts conductivity by about an order of magnitude; extrapolations imply less than ~40–70 wt ppm H₂O in lower-mantle bridgmanite — essentially dry compared with wetter upper-mantle reservoirs.

**Why it matters:** Lower-mantle water content was experimentally unconstrained; a dry bridgmanite reservoir reshapes models of deep water storage and recycling.

### 5. A proof of Gluck’s conjecture
Zhang (arXiv:2608.11525, 12 Aug) proves permutation- and module-action inequalities that imply Gluck’s 1985 conjecture for finite solvable groups: |G : F(G)| ≤ b(G)², relating the index of the Fitting subgroup to the largest irreducible character degree.

**Why it matters:** A four-decade open inequality in finite group theory is settled for solvable groups (preprint).
