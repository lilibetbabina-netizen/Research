# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-21

Friday’s Athens notes are led by 20 Aug journal VoRs: Science brings laser Mössbauer spectroscopy of the thorium-229 nuclear isomer in CaF₂ and pressure-made sheathed copper atom-chains, Physical Review X maps electron–ytterbium scattering with Rydberg molecules, and Physical Review Letters adds a heterostructure route to electron-doped nickelates, robust trapped-ion geometric gates, and a continuum theory for self-dual Higgs transitions. A 20 Aug maths preprint solves Milnor’s cartography problem on the sphere.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Optical Mössbauer spectroscopy of ²²⁹Th in CaF₂
Hiraki, Masuda, Takatori, Beeks, Grüneis and collaborators (Science, 20 Aug) use a vacuum-ultraviolet laser to probe the low-energy nuclear transition of thorium-229 doped in calcium fluoride. They find four doping sites, measure host-induced electric-field gradients, identify the two dominant microscopic configurations, and study isomer lifetimes and laser-induced quenching site by site.

**Why it matters:** Solid-state nuclear clocks need to know exactly how ²²⁹Th sits in its crystal; this turns that nuclear environment into a spectroscopic map.

### 2. Electron–ytterbium scattering from Rydberg molecules
Legrand, Wang, Hofferberth, Eiles and collaborators (Physical Review X, 20 Aug) record ultralong-range Rydberg molecules of ¹⁷⁴Yb over a wide range of principal quantum numbers. Matching spectra to Coulomb Green’s-function potentials yields electron–Yb phase shifts, including the s-wave scattering length and two p-wave shape resonances, and indicates that Yb⁻ is only a metastable resonance.

**Why it matters:** Ytterbium is a key divalent ultracold-atom platform; these scattering numbers are the kind of foundational data alkalis already have.

### 3. Heterostructuring toward electron-doped nickelates
Deng, Kitatani, Jiang and collaborators (Physical Review Letters, 20 Aug) use first-principles calculations to argue that intercalating wide-gap LaXO₃ layers into La₂NiO₄ donates electrons into Ni 3d states without chemical disorder, placing a La₂NiO₄:La₂AlO₄ heterostructure in a predicted optimal window for d-wave superconductivity with Tc above 50 K.

**Why it matters:** The electron-doped side of nickelate superconductivity has been hard to reach experimentally; a clean heterostructure knob would open that half of the phase diagram (theory).

### 4. Ion gates that tolerate hot motion
Bowers, Palani, Leibfried, Slichter and collaborators (Physical Review Letters, 20 Aug) entangle trapped ions by ramping both a state-dependent force and motional frequencies. Bell-state fidelities stay above 0.99 across a broad parameter range even with up to about ten phonons of motion.

**Why it matters:** Skipping ground-state cooling reduces overhead for quantum logic spectroscopy and larger ion-trap processors.

### 5. Continuum theory for self-dual toric-code transitions
Ji, Lanzetta, Zhou and Wang (Physical Review Letters, 20 Aug) propose an SO(4)₂,₋₂ Chern–Simons–Higgs theory for the continuous, duality-preserving transition out of a deformed toric code, and extend it to an SO(4)ₖ,₋ₖ family covering non-Abelian topological orders.

**Why it matters:** Self-dual topological quantum critical points have lacked a convincing continuum description; a labelled series organises that landscape.

### 6. Micrometer copper wires one atom wide
Zhang, Dong, Qiu and collaborators (Science, 20 Aug) compress β-copper phthalocyanine above about 21 GPa to make milligram-scale, micrometer-long carbon-sheathed copper single-atom chains with a 2.57 Å Cu–Cu spacing that can be exfoliated and studied.

**Why it matters:** Isolable, sheathed one-atom metal wires give condensed-matter and nanoelectronics a concrete one-dimensional platform.

### 7. Milnor’s cartography problem
Anton Petrunin (arXiv:2608.20265, 20 Aug) proves that among convex regions of fixed area on the sphere, the round disk requires the greatest distortion under cartographic projection to the plane.

**Why it matters:** A classical spherical-geometry extremal question now has a sharp answer (preprint).
