# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-21

Friday’s Athens scan uses primary sources from 20 August. A peer-reviewed spectroscopy study establishes Co₁/₄TaSe₂ as a layered altermagnetic platform. Fresh experiments image the spatial statistics of vacuum fluctuations in an analogue field and generate mutually phase-stable attosecond soft-X-ray pulse pairs. In mathematics, two preprints settle a spherical-map extremal problem posed by John Milnor and the exact asymptotic constant in a matrix inequality open since Schäffer’s 1970 bound.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Spin-split bands in a layered altermagnet
Sprague, Mondal, Sakhya and collaborators (Nature Communications, 20 Aug) identify Co₁/₄TaSe₂ as a layered altermagnet. Magnetic susceptibility finds type-A antiferromagnetic order at 178 K, while spin-resolved and conventional angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy show Fermi-surface spin splitting consistent with density-functional calculations. The reconstructed valence bands shift above the ordering temperature as the magnetic order is suppressed.

**Why it matters:** Altermagnets have no net magnetization but can still split electronic bands by spin. A layered compound with direct spectroscopic evidence provides a practical system in which to test the phase and its possible spin-transport behavior.

### 2. Seeing vacuum fluctuations in an analogue field
Zhang, Wang, Jiang and collaborators (arXiv:2608.20311, 20 Aug) use two coherently coupled spin states in a planar Bose–Einstein condensate to emulate a massive relativistic sine-Gordon field. Individual field images contain fluctuations across several length scales, with amplitudes matching the predicted vacuum-state statistics.

**Why it matters:** Vacuum fluctuations are usually detected through effects they cause. Imaging their spatial pattern in a controllable analogue system opens an experimental route to quantum-field regimes that are difficult to solve theoretically.

### 3. Controllable phase-stable attosecond X-ray pairs
Robles, Cesar, Driver and collaborators (arXiv:2608.20022, 20 Aug) produce two mutually phase-stable soft-X-ray pulses at LCLS-II. Their split-undulator method reuses electron microbunching to seed the second pulse, controls the delay in 250-attosecond steps, and tunes the relative phase.

**Why it matters:** Phase and timing control at soft-X-ray energies could support coherent pump–probe experiments on electronic and molecular motion at its natural timescale.

### 4. The round disk is the hardest spherical region to flatten
Anton Petrunin (arXiv:2608.20265, 20 Aug) resolves Milnor’s cartography problem: among convex regions of a fixed area on the sphere, the round disk requires the greatest distortion in any projection to the plane.

**Why it matters:** The theorem determines the extremal shape in a fundamental geometric optimization problem about how much distortion a flat map must introduce.

### 5. Schäffer’s matrix bound has exact growth √(en)
Houache, Szehr and Zarouf (arXiv:2608.20217, 20 Aug) prove that the optimal constant Sₙ in Schäffer’s determinant–inverse-norm inequality satisfies Sₙ/√n → √e. Thus the upper bound Schäffer proved in 1970 is asymptotically exact, including its constant.

**Why it matters:** The result closes a more-than-50-year gap and constructs Banach-space norms and matrices that approach the worst possible behavior.

## Watchlist

- **Wide-field infrared imaging with undetected photons (arXiv:2608.20015)** — reports room-temperature 6–10 μm imaging and roughly 100 times the background-limited infrared-photodetection limit; promising preprint awaiting review.
- **Additive arithmetic of linear orders (arXiv:2608.20309)** — solves a problem posed by Tarski in 1956 as part of a broader theory of ordered sums.
- **Lichiardopol’s digraph conjecture (arXiv:2608.20012)** — claims a full proof that sufficiently large minimum out-degree forces disjoint directed cycles of distinct lengths.
- **Dipolar supersolid structures and fiber-cavity atomic arrays (arXiv:2608.20327; 2608.20291)** — new experiments on competing two-dimensional supersolid order and site-resolved cavity QED.
