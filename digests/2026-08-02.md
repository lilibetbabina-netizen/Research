# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-02

Sunday’s notes cover a working accelerator-to-trap path for highly charged ions, a photonic architecture that finally folds nonlinearity into a scalable loop, strained-oxide spin texture, plus a watchlist quantum-advantage preprint and a foliated birational-geometry result.

## Top discoveries

### 1. First in-trap electron cooling of accelerator-produced highly charged ions
Rausch and colleagues at TU Darmstadt and GSI (Physical Review X; covered 1 Aug) complete the HITRAP chain for fully stripped argon: ions born near 0.3c are decelerated by about four orders of magnitude in kinetic energy, stored for seconds in a Penning cooling trap, and — for the first time — electron-cooled inside that trap.

**Why it matters:** Precision spectroscopy and materials experiments need slow, cold highly charged ions. Demonstrating the full path turns HITRAP into a usable platform rather than a promise.

### 2. Extensible photonic quantum computer with integrated nonlinearity (Clavina)
Yu, Sun, Chen and collaborators (Nature Photonics, 31 Jul) report Clavina: a time-bin, loop-based photonic processor that plugs an inline squeezer and a Kerr module into a programmable multi-core linear network. The same machine quasi-deterministically prepares optical Gottesman–Kitaev–Preskill (GKP) grid states and simulates a three-site Bose–Hubbard model with tunable interaction-to-tunnelling ratio beyond the hard-core limit.

**Why it matters:** Scalable photonic circuits have mostly been linear. Reusable nonlinear modules in one extensible architecture open a practical route to bosonic error correction and interacting many-body photonic simulation.

### 3. Strain-enabled spin texture in ultrathin RuO₂
Zhang et al. (Science Advances; covered 1 Aug) grow fully strained 2-nanometre RuO₂ films and map momentum-dependent spin textures with spin-resolved ARPES. Symmetry analysis rules out a purely nonmagnetic polar origin; the data fit weak ferromagnetism or altermagnetism. Measurements were taken near 15 K, so room-temperature behaviour is still open.

**Why it matters:** Whether RuO₂ is magnetic has been contested in bulk and relaxed films. Epitaxial strain in the ultrathin limit looks like a control knob for oxide spintronics without conventional ferromagnets.

### 4. Boundedness for polarized adjoint foliated surfaces
Chen and Zhu (arXiv:2607.28586, 30 Jul) run a minimal model program for klt adjoint foliated surfaces and prove boundedness for ε-lc polarized examples when the volume of the adjoint canonical divisor plus polarization is capped. They also get effective birationality, a uniform positive volume lower bound in the general-type case, and a discrete volume set for rank-one foliations on ε-lc Calabi–Yau adjoint foliated surfaces.

**Why it matters:** Classical surface boundedness theorems gain a foliated adjoint counterpart — structure that organises singular surface foliations (still a preprint).
