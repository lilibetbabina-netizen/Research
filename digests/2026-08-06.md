# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-06

Thursday’s notes centre on a dense 5 Aug Nature cluster — strong-field QED in a magnetar, erasure-biased quantum gates, Hubbard pseudogaps, solar photospheric mixing, and wafer-scale 2D superconductors — plus a 4 Aug algebraic-geometry preprint that falsifies the period–index conjecture.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Vacuum birefringence in polarized X-rays from a radio magnetar
Stewart, Dinh Thi, Younes and collaborators (Nature, 5 Aug) measure phase- and energy-resolved X-ray polarization from the radio magnetar 1E 1547.0−5408 with IXPE, NICER and Parkes. Soft X-ray polarization reaches ~65% phase-averaged at 2 keV and nearly 80% at some phases, while polarization angles follow the rotating-vector model. Standard non-refractive surface models struggle; vacuum birefringence in the magnetosphere — empty space acting like a birefringent medium in >10¹⁴ G fields — accounts for the signals.

**Why it matters:** Vacuum birefringence is a classic unconfirmed prediction of strong-field quantum electrodynamics. Magnetar polarimetry now offers a concrete astrophysical test of how extreme magnetic fields reshape light.

### 2. Entangling gate for dual-rail erasure qubits
Mehta, Teoh, Noh and colleagues at D-Wave Quantum (Nature, 5 Aug; print 6 Aug) demonstrate a ~500 ns Swap–Wait–Swap controlled-Z gate between dual-rail cavity qubits — qubits that flag photon loss as an erasure. The gate keeps the favourable noise hierarchy: roughly 0.5% erasure per gate, residual Pauli errors below 0.1%, and bit flips near 10⁻⁶, with surface-code simulations past predicted erasure-biased thresholds.

**Why it matters:** Erasure encoding only helps error correction if two-qubit gates preserve the hierarchy. A fast, bias-preserving CZ fills a missing hardware step toward scalable dual-rail architectures.

### 3. Pseudogap observed in a Fermi–Hubbard quantum simulator
Kendrick, Kale, Gang, Greiner and co-authors (Nature, 5 Aug) cool a cold-atom realization of the Hubbard model — the simplest lattice model of interacting electrons — enough to see a normal-metal to pseudogapped-metal crossover. Compressibility peaks at intermediate doping mark a thermodynamic anomaly line; lattice-modulation spectra lose low-energy weight, especially at antinodes, mapping a pseudogap phase diagram.

**Why it matters:** The pseudogap sits at the heart of cuprate physics but is tangled with real-material complexity. Seeing it cleanly in the Hubbard model itself strengthens the microscopic link between doped Mott insulators and high-temperature superconductivity precursors.

### 4. Ubiquitous Kelvin–Helmholtz instabilities mixing solar photospheric plasma
Kuridze, Wöger, van Noort, Solanki and collaborators (Nature, 5 Aug) use the 4 m Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope to resolve magnetized Kelvin–Helmholtz roll-ups — shear-driven vortices — at the edges of photospheric magnetic flux concentrations, confirming a long-predicted process. Matching simulations support vortex-driven flux braiding below the visible surface.

**Why it matters:** These tiny mixing layers help explain how turbulent convection injects and dissipates magnetic energy that heats and structures the solar atmosphere.

### 5. The period–index conjecture is false
Alexander Perry (arXiv:2608.03684, 4 Aug) builds a smooth projective threefold and a Brauer class with period 2 but index 8 over any algebraically closed field of characteristic zero — a counterexample to the period–index conjecture — and extends the gap to index 2^d in higher dimensions. The threefold case works even over the algebraic closure of the rationals.

**Why it matters:** Period–index bounds organize much of Brauer-group arithmetic geometry. An explicit Hodge-theoretic counterexample forces a sharper distinction between varieties where such control can still hold and those where it cannot (preprint).
