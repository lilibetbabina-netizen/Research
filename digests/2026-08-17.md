# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-17

Monday morning in Athens follows a quiet publication weekend, so this edition combines two papers published on 15–17 August with high-value results from the past week that had not entered earlier notes. The strongest evidence is an experimental qubit-reset protocol; the quantum-materials algorithm is a small hardware proof of concept, the early-black-hole interpretation is model-dependent, and both mathematics results are preprints.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Fixed-frequency transmons reset and remove leakage in 88 ns
Chen, Fors, Yan and collaborators demonstrate an unconditional reset protocol in a fixed-frequency transmon processor with tunable couplers. Excitations are moved from qubits into readout resonators and then into the feedline. The experiment resets the first excited state in 9 ns with about 0.19% error, resets multiple levels in 61 ns with about 0.79% error, and removes leakage in 5 ns; reset, leakage reduction and coupler clearing complete in 88 ns when operations run in parallel.

**Why it matters:** Repeated quantum error correction needs fast re-initialization and a way to remove population that escapes the two computational levels. This protocol uses an architecture already compatible with surface-code layouts.

### 2. A small DMFT materials calculation runs on quantum hardware
Hogan, Kökcü, Steckmann and co-authors compress state preparation and time evolution into a short circuit for dynamical mean-field theory (DMFT), a method used to model strongly interacting electrons. They show convergence in noise-free calculations and extract an impurity Green’s function on IBM hardware for one impurity coupled to three bath orbitals, using eight qubits plus one ancilla.

**Why it matters:** The impurity solver is a major DMFT bottleneck. This is a concrete hardware-tested route toward quantum-assisted materials modelling, but it is not yet a useful-scale quantum advantage.

### 3. JWST sees a plausible gas-enshrouded black hole at cosmic dawn
Naidu, Matthee, Katz and collaborators analyse MoM-BH*-1 as it appeared about 660 million years after the Big Bang. Its compact shape, broad hydrogen emission, deep hydrogen absorption and exceptionally strong Balmer break do not fit a normal stellar population. A simplified model in which a black hole is wrapped in extremely dense, turbulent and nearly dust-free gas reproduces the main spectral features.

**Why it matters:** Such an envelope could let early black holes grow rapidly and may explain some of JWST’s compact “little red dots” without invoking heavy dust. The conclusion is a physical interpretation of one source, not a direct image of the proposed structure.

### 4. Algebraic geometers construct a quintic surface with 18 cusps
Borisov and Rito build a six-dimensional family of degree-five surfaces whose typical member has 17 cusp singularities. They find 18-cusp examples over finite fields, lift one from arithmetic modulo 17 to characteristic zero, reconstruct exact coefficients over a degree-22 number field and verify that the resulting surface has exactly 18 ordinary cusps and no other singularities.

**Why it matters:** Explicit quintic surfaces were previously known with at most 17 cusps, while the upper bound is 20. The preprint narrows that classical gap and develops a computable route toward the remaining cases.

### 5. Homological conjectures align on virtually Gorenstein Artin algebras
Weiqing Li proves that, for a virtually Gorenstein Artin algebra, every module satisfying the semi-Gorenstein-projective vanishing conditions is actually Gorenstein projective. The theorem implies the Auslander–Gorenstein, Strong Nakayama and Tachikawa first conjectures in this class, with a further Auslander–Reiten consequence under an additional condition.

**Why it matters:** One structural theorem settles several related homological questions on a broad class of finite-dimensional algebras. It remains a preprint awaiting independent review.

## Watchlist

- **Hamiltonian-protected logical qubit (npj Quantum Information, 15 Aug)** — an alternating XX/YY ultrastrong-coupling chain suppresses pure dephasing to zero and halves relaxation in theory; an experimental implementation is still needed.
- **3R-NbSe₂ superconductivity (PRL accepted, 12 Aug)** — structural, transport, magnetic and thermodynamic measurements identify bulk non-centrosymmetric superconductivity and enhanced nonlinear response created by rhombohedral stacking.
- **Dense Seymour case (arXiv:2608.11530, 12 Aug)** — a short counting proof covers oriented graphs with n ≤ 2δ+2 and raises the minimum possible counterexample order; promising, but single-author and explicitly AI-assisted.
