# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-17

Monday’s Athens notes (after the weekend gap) centre on 15 Aug VoRs plus a 13 Aug PRL that phys.org highlighted midweek: Nature Communications reports an interface-localized giant bulk photovoltaic effect in MoS₂/Ta₂NiSe₅ and a mechanical map of Gofar transform-fault segmentation, Communications Physics realizes a disorder-driven topological Anderson staircase, Physical Review Letters argues nonlinear plasma dynamics erase resonant dark-photon heating bounds, Nature Photonics turns Hanbury Brown–Twiss correlations into an X-ray scintillator diagnostic, and a Friday maths preprint advances Erdős’s distinct-distances problem in three dimensions.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Giant bulk photovoltaic effect at a MoS₂/Ta₂NiSe₅ interface
Ma, Leng, Peng and collaborators (Nature Communications, 15 Aug) stack MoS₂ on Ta₂NiSe₅ in a cross-bar geometry so the overlap region breaks MoS₂’s inversion symmetry. The resulting bulk photovoltaic response is concentrated at that interface; orthogonal contacts help separate ordinary intralayer photocurrents from the interfacial shift-current channel strengthened by spontaneous charge transfer.

**Why it matters:** Bias-free photocurrent generation becomes an interfacial design problem in van der Waals heterostructures rather than a bulk crystal-chemistry accident.

### 2. Staircase topological Anderson transitions
Mannaï, Shu, Haddad and co-authors (Communications Physics, 15 Aug) show that increasing disorder can raise a topological invariant in steps and leave a nontrivial topological Anderson phase that survives into the strong-disorder regime instead of collapsing to a trivial Anderson insulator. They implement the staircase in a one-dimensional topolectrical circuit modelling nanotube physics.

**Why it matters:** Disorder, usually treated as topology’s enemy, becomes a knob for engineering robust topological phases.

### 3. Nonlinear plasmas reopen dark-photon searches
Hook, Huang and Shalaby (Physical Review Letters, 13 Aug) simulate resonant conversion of dark-photon dark matter into early-universe plasmons. Once Langmuir waves carry roughly the plasma’s thermal energy, ponderomotive nonlinearities scramble the resonance and stop further heating. Prior cosmological exclusions weaken by thousands to tens of millions across a huge mass range.

**Why it matters:** A leading light dark-matter candidate regains large regions of parameter space that resonant-heating arguments had seemed to close.

### 4. X-ray Hanbury Brown–Twiss spectroscopy
Katznelson, Kasten, Tziperman, Bucher and collaborators (Nature Photonics, 13 Aug) measure photon bunching in X-ray-driven scintillation light. From g⁽²⁾(τ) they extract emission timescales and photons-per-X-ray for many scintillator materials.

**Why it matters:** Timing and light yield dominate medical imaging and calorimetry performance; HBT supplies those intrinsic numbers from intensity correlations.

### 5. Mechanical controls on Gofar transform segmentation
Tan, Fan, Shearer, Behn and McGuire (Nature Communications, 15 Aug) publish 2,323 focal mechanisms from ocean-bottom seismometers on the western Gofar transform. Faulting style tracks segmentation: reverse and oblique-reverse events cluster in creeping barriers that bound the patches hosting magnitude 5.5+ earthquakes.

**Why it matters:** Links the creep-versus-quake pattern on oceanic transforms to a concrete along-strike mechanical map.

### 6. Distinct distances in three dimensions
Tidor, Yu and Zakharov (arXiv:2608.14454, 14 Aug) prove that N points in ℝ³ determine at least N^{2/3−o(1)} distinct distances.

**Why it matters:** A quantitative leap on Erdős’s distinct-distances problem beyond the plane (preprint).
