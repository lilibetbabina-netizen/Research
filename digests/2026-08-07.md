# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-07

Friday’s notes pick up fresh 6–7 Aug peer-reviewed physics — sunlight-driven entanglement, a tuneable lab-scale X-ray harmonic source, and multiparty superconducting secret sharing — plus two 5 Aug maths preprints on Turán densities and ball-quotient stacks.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Generating quantum entanglement from sunlight
Li, Brar, Fattahi, Boyd and colleagues (Optica, 6 Aug) generate polarization-entangled photon pairs by pumping spontaneous parametric down-conversion with concentrated outdoor sunlight. Tomography gives concurrence ≈ 0.91, Bell-state fidelity ≈ 0.94, and a CHSH value S ≈ 2.54 that violates Bell’s inequality; pair rates are of order 1600 s⁻¹ per mW of pump. An all-glass concentrator funnels Fresnel-collected sunlight into a fibre onto a millimetre crystal, showing that laser-grade coherence is not required for Bell-quality polarization entanglement.

**Why it matters:** Photonic quantum technologies lean heavily on power-hungry lasers. Sunlight-driven entanglement opens a path to lower-energy and space-deployable sources that use light already abundant in orbit.

### 2. Narrowband, wavelength-tuneable tabletop EUV–soft-X-ray harmonics
Popmintchev and collaborators (Communications Physics, 7 Aug) demonstrate a tabletop high-harmonic source that continuously shifts narrowband extreme-ultraviolet to soft-X-ray combs onto chosen absorption edges by jointly tuning spectral broadening, phase matching, waveguide dispersion, gas species and density. The combs keep laser-like coherence, narrow bandwidth and brightness, and are expected to arrive as trains of sub-300-attosecond pulses.

**Why it matters:** Resonant imaging and spectroscopy usually require free-electron lasers. A bright, continuously tuneable lab source would put attosecond-resonant experiments on magnetic nanostructures within reach of many more laboratories.

### 3. Quantum secret sharing on a triangular superconducting network
Yan, Zang, Grebel and co-authors (npj Quantum Information, 6 Aug) operate a three-node superconducting testbed whose edges are 1.3 m transmission lines. They show pairwise state transfer and entanglement, prepare genuine tripartite GHZ states, and implement quantum secret sharing of classical information with in-situ eavesdrop detection.

**Why it matters:** Multiparty quantum communication has mostly been photonic. A superconducting demonstration with built-in eavesdrop sensing is a concrete step toward multiparty distributed quantum processors.

### 4. Uniform Turán densities contain a terminal interval
Li, Liu and Pikhurko (arXiv:2608.04790, 5 Aug) prove that the set of uniform Turán densities of (possibly infinite) families of 3-graphs contains a whole interval [1−δ, 1] for some δ > 0 — so that set has positive Lebesgue measure and Hausdorff dimension 1.

**Why it matters:** Uniform Turán densities are a central organizing object in extremal hypergraph theory. Filling an interval near density one overturns sparse-set intuitions about which densities can actually arise (preprint).

### 5. A characterization of ball quotient stacks
Chowdhury, Costantini and Patel (arXiv:2608.04688, 5 Aug) characterize which smooth proper Deligne–Mumford stacks arise as compactifications of ball quotient stacks, and show every ball quotient admits a compactification whose boundary is a disjoint union of abelian-variety quotient stacks — extending Deng–Cadorel with log non-abelian Hodge theory.

**Why it matters:** Ball quotients sit at the heart of complex-hyperbolic geometry. Knowing which stacky boundaries are allowed ties uniformization more tightly to modern Hodge theory (preprint).
