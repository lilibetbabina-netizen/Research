# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-01

Friday’s digest centers on topological quantum information—more stable non-Abelian error correction and a watched Majorana-like braid—plus zero-field kagome thermoelectrics and a geometric-measure-theory preprint on singular area-minimizers.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Intrinsic heralding boosts non-Abelian quantum error correction
Jing, Sala, Jiang, and Verresen (Physical Review Letters; covered 30–31 Jul) argue that non-Abelian topological order need not be less stable than Abelian codes. Instead of adding flag qubits, their decoders treat imperfect anyon fusion products as intrinsic heralds of noise. When errors are dominated by one non-Abelian anyon type, that built-in information raises the noise threshold by about 38% versus comparable Abelian systems.

**Why it matters:** Non-Abelian anyons are prized for computation but long assumed costly for stability. Intrinsic heralding flips that trade-off on paper—pending experimental tests.

### 2. Real-space braiding of Majorana-like zero modes
Ma and colleagues (Nature Communications, 31 Jul) map the full real-space trajectory of braiding Majorana-like zero modes in an acoustic artificial lattice. Boundary-bound modes are moved along the sample edge by modulating the Kekulé phase; acoustic-field movies show the braid path and the phase accumulation expected from the exchange.

**Why it matters:** Braiding statistics sit at the heart of topological quantum gates. Watching an analog braid unfold in space makes those abstract anyonic operations experimentally concrete.

### 3. Goniopolar transverse thermoelectricity in kagome metals
Hu et al. (Nature Materials, 31 Jul) exploit kagome flat bands and van Hove singularities to produce goniopolarity—opposite carrier polarity along different crystal axes—so a temperature gradient yields a transverse voltage with no magnetic field. In LuCo₆Ge₆ they report 18.4 μV K⁻¹ transverse thermopower and 105 A m⁻¹ K⁻¹ transverse Peltier conductivity at room temperature, far above typical zero-field anomalous-Nernst benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** Transverse thermoelectrics usually need magnets and suffer stray-field interference. A materials-design route from kagome fermiology could simplify waste-heat recovery and solid-state cooling.

### 4. White’s generic-smoothness question for area-minimizers
Liu (arXiv:2607.24735, 27–28 Jul) proves that mod-2 area-minimizing submanifolds are not generically smooth except in the classical cases of geodesics, minimal surfaces, and minimal hypersurfaces. Singular sets can retain Hausdorff dimension at least d−3 for open sets of metrics, and the Veronese cone over ℝP² is shown to be mod-2 area-minimizing.

**Why it matters:** Settles (negatively, beyond the known low-dimensional cases) whether generic metric perturbations erase singularities of area-minimizing cycles—core geometric measure theory, still preprint-stage.
