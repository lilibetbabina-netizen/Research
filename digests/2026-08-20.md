# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-20

Thursday's Athens digest covers four results from the 18–19 August primary-source window: two new ways to probe and control condensed matter at microscopic scales, a star whose orbit may expose the spin of the Milky Way's central black hole, and a combinatorial extension of a geometric signature formula.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Vacuum fluctuations raise NbSe₂'s superconducting transition
Wang, Cardoso, Yang and collaborators embedded layered NbSe₂ in a split-ring cavity resonator. They observed an increased superconducting critical temperature, together with sharp increases in critical current and field near the transition. Calculations are consistent with fluctuating cavity modes hybridizing with electronic degrees of freedom and lowering the superconducting state's energy.

**Why it matters:** The experiment shows that even a cavity's electromagnetic vacuum can tune a collective material phase. It is a proof of principle for non-invasive control of superconducting and quantum devices, not yet a general recipe for high-temperature superconductivity.

### 2. An atomic-pair interferometer reads local bond motion
Tabata, Seki, Susi and collaborators focused an electron probe between two silicon atomic columns just 1.36 Å apart. The columns acted like a double slit: their interference fringes survived from 300 K to 900 K because neighbouring atoms vibrated in a correlated way. Matching the fringes to phonon simulations extracted directional correlations linked to local bond stiffness.

**Why it matters:** Most measurements of lattice vibrations average over large regions. This method could instead examine bond-scale dynamics at defects, interfaces and other sites that govern heat transport and material behaviour.

### 3. S301 could reveal the spin of Sagittarius A*
Abd El Dayem, Abuter, Aimar and collaborators report a faint star on an 8.7-year, highly eccentric orbit around the Milky Way's central black hole. S301 passes within roughly 140 Schwarzschild radii and reaches about 25,000 km s⁻¹. Simulations suggest that continued infrared interferometry and future extremely-large-telescope spectroscopy could resolve spin-driven frame dragging within about a decade for favourable spin configurations.

**Why it matters:** A stellar-orbit measurement would provide a comparatively direct test of the Kerr description of a rotating massive black hole. This remains a forecast, not a spin detection: radial-velocity data and careful modelling of nearby masses are still needed.

### 4. Hodge geometry extends to arbitrary convex polytopes
Jacob B. Wood's 18 August preprint uses combinatorial intersection cohomology to extend the Maxim-Schuermann signature formula from rational polytopes to all convex polytopes. It also develops a polytope version of the Hodge index theorem, linking the combinatorics of convex shapes to structures familiar from algebraic geometry.

**Why it matters:** Removing the rational-coordinate restriction broadens a geometric invariant into purely combinatorial convex geometry. The result is a preprint and awaits peer review.

## Watchlist

- **Polaritonic flat-band bound states in a 2-D magnet** — Shen, Ren, Yuan and collaborators experimentally produced wide-angle, nearly dispersionless light–matter states in a CrSBr metasurface. The peer-reviewed result may enable new topological and nonlinear photonics, but applications remain prospective.
- **Quantum stability of a fuzzy-sphere black-hole horizon** — Chong-Sun Chu's preprint finds one-loop stabilization in a large-N matrix model. It is mathematically structured but its connection to real black holes remains model-dependent.
