# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-20

Thursday’s Athens notes centre on 19 Aug Nature VoRs: a Rydberg-atom quantum simulator resolves conformal field theory spectra at Ising and tricritical Ising critical points, cavity vacuum modes enhance NbSe₂ superconductivity, STEM turns neighbouring silicon columns into an atomic double slit, and a newly found Galactic-centre star is sensitive to the spin of Sagittarius A*. Physical Review Letters adds a concrete JUNO route to the solar MSW transition and an 18 Aug holographic check of a-maximization, while a 19 Aug maths preprint pushes the Erdős matching conjecture closer to its conjectured linear threshold.

## Top discoveries

### 1. CFT energy ladders in a Rydberg simulator
Sun, Le, Naus, Endres, Alicea and collaborators (Nature, 19 Aug) tune a Rydberg-atom chain through quantum phase transitions described by the Ising and tricritical Ising conformal field theories. Many-body modulation spectroscopy on chains of up to ~35 atoms recovers the predicted universal energy ratios, sorts excitations by reflection parity, and measures a dynamical structure factor linked to an Ising conformal correlator.

**Why it matters:** Universality classes stop being abstract labels when their full low-lying spectrum can be read out experimentally in a programmable quantum simulator.

### 2. Cavity vacuum fluctuations raise NbSe₂ Tc
Wang, Cardoso, Cheng and collaborators (Nature, 19 Aug) place superconducting NbSe₂ inside a split-ring cavity and observe a higher critical temperature, with critical current and field rising sharply near the transition. Theory points to hybridisation between electrons and fluctuating cavity vacuum modes that lowers the superconducting-state energy.

**Why it matters:** Enhancing superconductivity without doping or continuous drives would give materials a noninvasive control knob relevant to quantum devices.

### 3. Double-slit interference at 1.36 Å
Tabata, Seki, Susi, Ishikawa and Shibata (Nature, 19 Aug) delocalise a STEM electron probe over two adjacent Si [110] columns and record Young-type fringes. Visibility that survives from 300 K to 900 K encodes correlated thermal vibrations, yielding anisotropic bond stiffness for that atomic pair.

**Why it matters:** Atomic-scale interferometry turns wave–particle duality into a local probe of lattice dynamics that bulk diffraction cannot isolate.

### 4. Star S301 feels Sagittarius A*’s spin
The GRAVITY/Sgr A* collaboration (Nature, 19 Aug) reports main-sequence star S301 on an 8.7-year, highly eccentric orbit that peaks near 25,000 km s⁻¹. Its pericentre is close enough that Kerr (spin) corrections of order β³ become accessible to current interferometry and future ELT spectroscopy.

**Why it matters:** Orbits have tested Schwarzschild gravity around the Galactic-centre black hole; a spin-sensitive star opens a path to measuring how fast Sgr A* rotates.

### 5. JUNO’s route to the solar MSW transition
Nairat, Beacom, Kelly and Li (Physical Review Letters, 19 Aug) show how suppressing muon-induced spallation backgrounds could let JUNO measure the energy-dependent Mikheyev–Smirnov–Wolfenstein transition in solar neutrinos at >4σ within a decade.

**Why it matters:** Matter-induced flavor conversion is a pillar of neutrino mixing that still lacks a conclusive energy-dependent solar detection.

### 6. Holographic a-maximization beyond two-derivative gravity
Hristov, Khandelwal, Pang and Tartaglino-Mazzucchelli (Physical Review Letters, 18 Aug) evaluate higher-derivative five-dimensional gauged supergravity on AdS₅ and find that the holographic result still matches the dual CFT₄ trial a-anomaly, so a-maximization continues to pick the correct superconformal R-symmetry.

**Why it matters:** The AdS/CFT dictionary remains predictive when the bulk includes more realistic higher-derivative corrections.

### 7. Tightening the Erdős matching window
Cao, Liu and Zhang (arXiv:2608.19118, 19 Aug) prove that for fixed uniformity k≥2 and large matching number s, once n≥(k+1)s the largest k-uniform family with matching number ≤s is the family of all edges meeting a fixed s-set. The proven linear coefficient drops from (5k−2)/3 to k+1.

**Why it matters:** That is closer to the conjectured threshold where the “hitting” construction beats the competing “concentrate on few vertices” extremal example (preprint).
