# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-12

Wednesday’s strongest verified results come from the 11 Aug publication cycle: a tighter null test for a permanent muon electric dipole moment, two independent optical views into the dynamics of electron crystals, a compact architecture for highly complex spectral processing, and mathematical advances that identify a sharp regularity obstruction and break a quadratic information barrier.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Fermilab tightens the direct muon electric-dipole bound
The Muon g−2 Collaboration searched 2.34 billion reconstructed positron decays from its 2019–2020 data for the vertical oscillation that a permanent muon electric dipole moment (EDM) would create. The result is consistent with zero and sets \(|d_\mu|<1.10\times10^{-19}\ e\,{\rm cm}\) at 95% confidence, improving the previous direct limit of \(1.8\times10^{-19}\ e\,{\rm cm}\).

**Why it matters:** A nonzero EDM would reveal time-reversal and CP violation beyond the Standard Model, potentially informing the origin of the Universe’s matter–antimatter imbalance. This preprint reports a tighter direct, comparatively model-independent muon test.

### 2. Two experiments turn Wigner-crystal motion into optical signals
Two independent Nature Physics teams observe Wigner polarons in charge-tunable monolayer WSe₂: hybrid excitations produced when light-created excitons couple to collective motion in an electron crystal. Wang, Menzel, Pichler and colleagues resolve polaron resonances and connect them to the crystal’s spin state. Zhang, Gu, Adlong and colleagues independently distinguish static and dynamical optical signatures, control spins with light and optically melt the crystal.

**Why it matters:** Wigner crystals—ordered lattices formed by repelling electrons—have mainly been studied through static order and transport. Optical access provides a fast, non-contact probe of their internal dynamics and a route to manipulating correlated quantum phases.

### 3. Eleven reflective nodes compress hyperspectral processing
He, Tu, Wang and collaborators use partially reflective, recursively designed nodes so light revisits many paths inside one integrated circuit. An 11-node waveguide attains spectral complexity comparable to at least 100 cascaded Mach–Zehnder interferometers; arrays reconstruct spectra with sub-picometre resolution across an 800 nm span and monitor optical and radio-frequency signals together.

**Why it matters:** Reusing reflections produces rich spectral responses without a proportionally deep device, providing a compact architecture for broadband spectroscopy, sensing and photonic signal processing.

### 4. One-half Hölder continuity is not enough for maximal parabolic regularity
Lukas Niebel constructs a diffusion coefficient that is uniformly elliptic, real, scalar, one-half Hölder-continuous in time and arbitrarily close to the ordinary heat equation’s constant coefficient. Even so, a suitable regular forcing produces a variational solution whose time derivative is not square-integrable. The counterexample extends to every spatial dimension and bounded domain.

**Why it matters:** The preprint confirms the Auscher–Egert conjecture and identifies a sharp obstruction for time-dependent parabolic PDEs: endpoint continuity alone cannot guarantee maximal \(L^2\) regularity.

### 5. Quantum-entropy estimation becomes provably subquadratic
Minbo Gao and Qisheng Wang give the first estimator that needs \(o(d^2)\) copies to estimate the von Neumann entropy of an unknown \(d\)-dimensional quantum state at fixed error. Its leading sample cost is \(O(d^2\log^2\!\log d/\log^2 d)\), using new tools that treat large and small eigenvalues separately.

**Why it matters:** Von Neumann entropy measures quantum randomness and entanglement and appears in Hamiltonian learning and thermal-state preparation. Breaking the quadratic sample barrier lowers a longstanding information cost, pending peer review.

- **Kohayakawa and clique-covering conjectures (arXiv:2608.11132)** — Bo Ning’s preprint proves the Kneser-graph growth conjecture and settles asymptotics for clique covers of path and cycle complements.
