# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-12

Wednesday’s notes are dominated by 11 Aug VoRs: two Nature Physics papers open an optical window onto zero-field Wigner-crystal dynamics, a long-awaited PRL publishes 420 km memory–memory entanglement, CO polarization maps toroidal fields in a protostellar wind, an acoustic lattice shows non-annihilating exceptional-point collisions on a nonorientable Brillouin zone, and an 11 Aug preprint resolves Kohayakawa’s growth-rate conjecture.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Spectroscopy of Wigner crystal polarons in a WSe₂ monolayer
Wang, Menzel, Pichler, Knüppel, Knap, Smoleński and collaborators (Nature Physics, 11 Aug) find new cryogenic reflectance resonances in charge-tunable monolayer WSe₂ that they identify as Wigner crystal polarons — excitons dressed by collective excitations of a zero-field electronic Wigner crystal. Unlike umklapp replicas that mainly track the lattice constant, polaron energies also depend on hybridization with attractive exciton–polarons, and the resonances give an optical handle on the crystal’s spin. A same-day companion paper (Zhang, Gu, Zhou, Imamoglu and co-authors) independently observes Wigner polarons, demonstrates all-optical spin control with no external field, and shows that umklapp and polaron resonances melt differently under optical excitation.

**Why it matters:** Static Wigner order has been seen in several 2-D platforms; an optical interface to collective dynamics and spin, without a magnetic field, enables frequency-domain and ultrafast control of correlation-driven electronic crystals.

### 2. Entangling quantum memories through 420 km of fibre
Luo, Bao, Pan and collaborators (Physical Review Letters, VoR 11 Aug; preprint arXiv:2504.05660) herald entanglement between two atomic-ensemble memories over 420 km of fibre via the DLCZ protocol. Frequency conversion into the telecom S-band and a dual phase-locking scheme keep the relative phase stable; the heralded success probability beats the repeaterless (PLOB) bound for direct entanglement distribution over the same channel.

**Why it matters:** Stored matter–matter entanglement is a core quantum-repeater ingredient. Exceeding the direct-transmission capacity over hundreds of kilometres of fibre is a concrete step toward quantum networks beyond metropolitan scale.

### 3. Dominant toroidal magnetic fields in a protostellar outflow
Ching, Li, Zhang, Girart and colleagues (Nature Communications, 11 Aug) use CO polarization toward NGC 1333 IRAS 4A to map magnetic geometry at hundreds of astronomical units. Fields lie perpendicular to the outflow axis and follow the wind’s rotation — toroidal geometry — at a few milligauss, strong enough to collimate and accelerate the outflow; the curl of the plane-of-sky field correlates linearly with line-of-sight current density.

**Why it matters:** Magneto-centrifugal models predicted toroidal hoop-stress collimation, but direct confirmation in a protostellar wind was missing. These data support magnetically driven, rotating outflows in star formation.

### 4. Non-annihilating exceptional-point collisions on nonorientable manifolds
Wang, Zhang, Tong, Qiu and co-authors (Nature Communications, 11 Aug) show in theory and in a mixed-dimensional non-Hermitian acoustic lattice that two exceptional points created from a hybrid point can re-encounter and form a vortex point instead of annihilating. Band-braid and Berry-phase measurements, plus a path-squeezing test, link the effect to braid-charge inversion when an exceptional point crosses a nonorientable boundary.

**Why it matters:** Nonorientability of momentum space becomes a tool for exceptional topology, enabling nontrivial degeneracy collisions even in simple two-band Abelian systems.

### 5. Kohayakawa’s conjecture and clique coverings of path and cycle complements
Ning (arXiv:2608.11132, 11 Aug) proves a lower bound that implies \(\sup_s w(s)^{1/s}=4\) for the longest induced path in a bipartite Kneser-type graph \(G_s\), as conjectured by Kohayakawa (1991). The same bounds settle the 1985 de Caen–Gregory–Pullman conjecture that the clique-cover numbers of the complements of paths and cycles are \(\log_2 n+\Theta(\log_2\log_2 n)\).

**Why it matters:** Two long-standing questions in extremal set theory and graph covering are resolved (preprint).
