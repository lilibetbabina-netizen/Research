# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-08

Saturday’s notes pick up a dense 7 Aug peer-reviewed cluster — correlation-driven soft-X-ray harmonics, duality-defect transmission that clarifies a monopole paradox, ultrafast diagnostics of competing charge-density waves, and a structural result on correlators versus wave functions — plus a 6 Aug maths preprint that reads knot invariants through Coxeter–Catalan combinatorics.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Correlated electrons push high-harmonic X-rays past the single-electron cutoff
Wang, Yan, de las Heras, Popmintchev and colleagues (Nature Photonics, 7 Aug) drive helium with an intense UV laser and observe a weak secondary high-harmonic plateau that continues past the usual single-active-electron cutoff (~120 eV) into the water window near 280 eV. They interpret the plateau as double-electron recombination of strongly correlated electron pairs, with a cutoff that scales as ~5.5 times the ponderomotive energy instead of the textbook ~3.2. Ellipticity scans and time-dependent Schrödinger calculations support a coherent rescattering origin rather than incoherent plasma light.

**Why it matters:** Tabletop attosecond sources have been framed as single-electron upconverters. A correlation-driven soft-X-ray channel both extends their spectral reach and turns high-harmonic spectra into probes of ultrafast electron–electron entanglement.

### 2. Perfect transmission through duality defects resolves a monopole paradox
Ueda, Vander Linden, Verstraete and co-authors (Nature Physics, 7 Aug) study wavepackets crossing topological interfaces in quantum spin systems with non-invertible symmetries. Transmission is perfect: the particle converts into a non-local string-like excitation in a twisted sector. Realizing a Kramers–Wannier duality defect makes the Callan–Rubakov puzzle concrete — the “missing” outgoing state after monopole scattering reappears as a fog-wisp string excitation that modern generalized-symmetry language had predicted.

**Why it matters:** A four-decade high-energy paradox becomes a movie one can simulate on a spin chain, and a precise laboratory language for topological interfaces and twisted sectors.

### 3. Competing charge-density waves recover by different mechanisms
Su, Lv, Zong and collaborators (Nature Physics, 7 Aug) track the two competing charge-density-wave orders in erbium tritelluride (ErTe₃) with time- and angle-resolved photoemission after a light pulse. The dominant order reforms smoothly and uniformly, like a second-order transition; the weaker, poorly understood order reappears by nucleation and growth of isolated pockets, like a first-order freeze. Time-dependent Ginzburg–Landau modelling matches the contrast.

**Why it matters:** Many quantum materials host overlapping electronic phases. Distinct recovery pathways give experimentalists a time-domain handle on which interactions drive which transitions — information that steady-state spectra alone blur.

### 4. Equal-time correlators are simpler than wave functions
Arkani-Hamed, Glew and Vazão (Physical Review Letters, 7 Aug) explain a growing observation that equal-time correlators are analytically cleaner than wave functions: correlators integrate over all of spacetime, while wave functions integrate only over a half. That geometric difference removes some singularities, simplifies factorization, forces the first subleading Laurent coefficient around every pole to vanish, and yields a differential operator that rebuilds the near–total-energy expansion from scattering amplitudes.

**Why it matters:** Experiments and cosmological surveys measure correlators, not wave functions. Knowing why correlators are structurally simpler sharpens analytic control over amplitudes and early-universe observables.

### 5. Decategorifying knot invariants via noncrossing combinatorics
Defant and Williams (arXiv:2608.06225, 6 Aug) reverse the usual categorification programme: they compute the (a, z=0)-HOMFLYPT polynomial as a factorization problem in reflection groups, linking dual braid generators and Hecke images of pure braids to Coxeter–Catalan combinatorics. Along the way they give a new proof that the noncrossing partition lattice is EL-shellable, read its homotopy type from the inverse full twist, and supply the first reflection subword models for rational noncrossing Catalan objects in crystallographic type.

**Why it matters:** Knot polynomials gain combinatorial models that connect directly to modern Coxeter–Catalan geometry, offering calculational routes that do not require building the full categorified invariant first (preprint).
