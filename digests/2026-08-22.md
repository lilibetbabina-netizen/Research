# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-22

Saturday’s Athens notes lean on Friday (21 Aug) journal VoRs after a quiet Saturday: Physical Review X reports large free-space squeezed cat states and collective copper hopping in a superionic crystal, Nature Communications realises erratic non-Hermitian skin localization in an acoustic lattice, Physical Review Letters pushes conservative black-hole scattering to fifth post-Minkowskian and second self-force order and tests when pure states stay thermal under LOCC, PRX Quantum publishes concrete Shor resource estimates for secp256k1 blockchains, and a 20 Aug maths preprint settles the asymptotic constant in Schäffer’s matrix inequality.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Large squeezed cat states on free-space pulses
Caron, Simon, Basset, Journet and Tualle-Brouri (Physical Review X, 21 Aug) mix single- and two-photon Fock states on a tunable beam splitter and herald with homodyne detection to make squeezed odd cat states on free-space optical pulses. They reach amplitude α = 2.47 with squeezing z = 0.56, see three clear negative Wigner regions, and report fidelity 0.53 with the target state — larger than prior free-space values they cite.

**Why it matters:** Bigger non-Gaussian optical states are a practical bottleneck for continuous-variable quantum computing; higher cat amplitudes help temporal breeding protocols.

### 2. Collective ion hops in a superionic crystal
Lin, Gupta, Ouyang, Stewart and collaborators (Physical Review X, 21 Aug) use single-crystal neutron scattering on Cu₇PS₆ to map four-dimensional correlations of copper motion. Coherent quasielastic and diffuse scattering show short-range dynamical correlations among hopping Cu ions, and machine-learned molecular dynamics matches the data while identifying the collective hopping motif.

**Why it matters:** Superionic electrolytes were long thought to rely on correlated jumps; this is direct experimental evidence of that collective picture.

### 3. Erratic non-Hermitian skin localization
Zhong, Kim, Longhi and Jing (Nature Communications, 21 Aug) build a disordered acoustic Hatano–Nelson chain and observe erratic non-Hermitian skin localization: macroscopic, disorder-dependent localization at irregular bulk sites with subexponential decay, plus ballistic wave-packet transport diagnosed from the full complex spectrum.

**Why it matters:** Skin effects usually pin waves to edges; this regime shows disorder can park localization deep in the bulk.

### 4. Black-hole scattering at 5PM–2SF
Driesse, Jakobsen, Mogull, Plefka and collaborators (Physical Review Letters, 21 Aug) compute conservative scattering angle and impulse for classical black holes at fifth post-Minkowskian and second self-force order with worldline quantum field theory, reducing hundreds of four-loop master integrals and tracking a spurious velocity divergence that must cancel against radiative memory.

**Why it matters:** Gravitational-wave templates need these high-order building blocks for precision waveform models.

### 5. Shor resource estimates for elliptic-curve crypto
Babbush, Zalcman, Gidney, Neven and collaborators (PRX Quantum, 21 Aug) estimate that breaking secp256k1’s 256-bit elliptic-curve discrete logarithm with Shor’s algorithm needs either ≤1200 logical qubits and ≤90 million Toffoli gates or ≤1450 logical qubits and ≤70 million Toffoli gates, validated via a zero-knowledge proof and paired with mitigation discussion for blockchains.

**Why it matters:** Concrete qubit and gate budgets turn “quantum threat” timelines into engineering migration plans for digital money.

### 6. Thermal equivalence under LOCC work
Yada, Yoshioka and Sagawa (Physical Review Letters, 21 Aug) give criteria for when many-body pure states that look thermal under local operations remain thermal when classical communication is allowed, tying the answer to multipartite entanglement structure.

**Why it matters:** Quantum thermodynamics needs operational tests of when pure states still behave like heat baths once parties can talk.

### 7. Exact asymptotic for Schäffer’s matrix inequality
Houache, Szehr and Zarouf (arXiv:2608.20217, 20 Aug) prove that Schäffer’s constant Sₙ for invertible operators on n-dimensional complex Banach spaces satisfies lim Sₙ/√n = √e, so the classic √(en) upper bound is asymptotically sharp including the constant.

**Why it matters:** A fifty-year gap in quantitative Banach-space geometry closes with a sharp large-n constant (preprint).
