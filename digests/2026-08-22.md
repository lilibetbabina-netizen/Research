# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-22

Saturday’s Athens window is short but substantive. Three peer-reviewed papers from 20–22 August advance attosecond measurement, unconventional superconductivity and layered spin materials. With no 21 August arXiv submission cycle, the mathematics scan extends to 18 August and treats its unreviewed proofs more cautiously.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Bright, isolated light pulses lasting 18 attoseconds
Gao, Hasan, Liang and collaborators use high-order harmonic generation driven by a post-compressed ytterbium laser to produce isolated 18 ± 2 attosecond pulses. Their 50–320 eV spectrum reaches the carbon K-edge, with a generation-stage flux above 10¹² photons per second. Angle-resolved photoelectron streaking in helium checks the pulse from opposite electron-momentum directions.

**Why it matters:** One atomic unit of time is 24.2 attoseconds. A bright tabletop pulse shorter than that can follow electron motion and inner-shell relaxation on their natural timescales.

### 2. Pressure separates superconducting phases in UTe₂
Zou, Shi, Qiu and collaborators use point-contact spectroscopy to track the heavy-fermion superconductor UTe₂ under pressure. Andreev bound states strongly suggest an odd-kz component in both ambient- and high-pressure order parameters, while modelling distinguishes the phases by the relative weights of pz- and px(y)-wave pairing.

**Why it matters:** UTe₂ is a leading candidate for spin-triplet and topological superconductivity. A symmetry-sensitive signal that evolves under pressure sharply narrows its possible pairing states, though it does not identify one unique order parameter.

### 3. A layered material with altermagnetic spin splitting
Sprague, Mondal, Sakhya and collaborators identify Co₁/₄TaSe₂ as a layered altermagnet. Magnetic measurements show type-A antiferromagnetic order up to 178 K, while spin-resolved photoemission resolves the predicted spin-split electronic bands at the Fermi surface.

**Why it matters:** Altermagnets combine no net magnetisation with spin-split bands. This layered example gives researchers a comparatively high-temperature platform for testing that unusual combination and possible spin-transport effects.

### 4. Banach’s isometric conjecture completed over complex spaces
Antonio Acuaviva and Tomasz Kania prove that if all fixed-dimensional complex subspaces of a complex normed space are mutually isometric, then the full norm must come from a Hermitian inner product. Their preprint closes the remaining complex cases, proves a quaternionic counterpart and removes convexity from the underlying star-body result.

**Why it matters:** The theorem closes a long-running rigidity question: identical geometry in every subspace of one dimension forces Hilbert-space geometry globally (preprint).

### 5. Tarski’s additive problem for linear orders
Garrett Ervin and Eric Paul resolve a problem posed by Alfred Tarski in 1956. For arbitrary linear orders A and B, an identity of the form nA + mB ≅ kB + lA, with positive integer coefficients, forces A + B ≅ B + A. Their preprint also characterizes all additively commuting pairs of linear orders.

**Why it matters:** Addition of order types is normally noncommutative. The theorem closes a 70-year-old question about when a mixed arithmetic identity forces commutation (preprint).

## Watchlist

- **Schäffer’s matrix inequality** — Houache, Szehr and Zarouf claim the exact five-decade asymptotic Sₙ/√n → √e and give explicit near-extremal matrices and norms; substantial but still an unreviewed preprint.
- **Transferable neural wavefunctions** — Orbformer reaches chemical accuracy on reported bond-breaking benchmarks after pretraining across 22,000 structures; promising quantum chemistry at the edge of this digest’s scope.
- **Lichiardopol’s distinct-cycle conjecture** — Albrechtsen and Steiner prove the general directed-graph statement and a vertex-weighted extension; credible but displaced by the older Tarski problem in today’s top set.
- **Light-ion collective flow at CMS** — O+O and Ne+Ne collisions constrain how nuclear geometry maps into particle flow, but the Physical Review Letters paper appeared on 17 August and the 21 August coverage is press lag.
