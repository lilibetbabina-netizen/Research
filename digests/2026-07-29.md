# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-07-29

A precision particle-physics null result, a sharp probability-theory proof, and a materials imaging breakthrough frame Wednesday’s digest.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Ultracold neutrons do not oscillate into a mirror world
Researchers at PSI trapped ultracold neutrons in a magnetically controlled vessel and counted survivors after ~200 s storage, repeating the cycle until roughly 25 billion neutrons were measured. Scanning field strength and direction found no neutron↔mirror-neutron oscillation signal. Prior anomaly regions are excluded at about 99.98% confidence (Physical Review Letters, online 28 Jul 2026).

**Why it matters:** Mirror neutrons were a candidate explanation for neutron-lifetime puzzles and a speculative dark-matter channel; closing that window steers theory elsewhere.

### 2. Feige’s conjecture proved (δ = 1 case)
Nie and Wei (arXiv:2607.24528, 27 Jul) prove that for n independent nonnegative random variables with mean one, the probability their sum is less than n+1 is at least (n/(n+1))^n ≥ 1/e — Feige’s sharp constant. The argument uses the Vlassis–Thomas “merger” function K_n. The broader conjecture for every δ > 0 remains open.

**Why it matters:** Distribution-free lower-tail bounds of this kind feed randomized algorithms, combinatorics, and concentration inequalities; the sharp 1/e constant was open for two decades.

### 3. Nanoscale polar textures imaged in quantum-paraelectric SrTiO₃
Cryogenic scanning transmission electron microscopy (Nature, 22 Jul 2026) shows polar nanodomains in SrTiO₃ that organize into periodic textures below about 105 K, then fragment into smaller uncorrelated clusters once the quantum-paraelectric regime sets in near 40 K — rather than forming long-range ferroelectric order.

**Why it matters:** SrTiO₃’s huge permittivity, near-ferroelectricity, and unconventional superconductivity may trace to this real-space polar order/disorder landscape.
