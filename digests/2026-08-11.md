# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-11

Tuesday’s primary-source scan found five substantial results from 10 August. All are new announcements or preprints, so the claims below remain medium-confidence until they receive broader expert or peer-review scrutiny. The most consequential mathematics claim comes with a public formal proof artifact, while the two physics selections report experiments rather than proposals.

## Top discoveries

### 1. A claimed 67.2% lower bound for zeta zeros on the critical line
Anthropic says Claude combined earlier analytic-number-theory work to raise the proven lower bound for the fraction of nontrivial Riemann-zeta zeros on the critical line from 41.6% to 67.2%. The accompanying paper is “More than two thirds of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function lie on the critical line.” Anthropic mathematicians checked the argument, two external experts examined it on short notice, and a public Lean 4 formalization covers the paper’s main theorems without unfinished `sorry` placeholders. This is not a proof of the Riemann hypothesis.

**Why it matters:** Zeta zeros encode fine structure in the distribution of prime numbers. If the proof survives broad specialist scrutiny, this is a large quantitative advance; the formalization makes its logical core directly auditable.

### 2. Small 3-D compressible flows are proved to form shocks
Klainerman, Wang, Yang & Yu prove that every smooth, sufficiently small, compactly supported disturbance of a constant state in three-dimensional irrotational compressible flow must break down in finite time. No symmetry, explicit compression or non-degeneracy condition is imposed. They identify the breakdown as shock formation and recover the lifespan predicted by radiation-field analysis in the small-data limit.

**Why it matters:** The theorem establishes, for this model, that dispersion cannot save even tiny localized disturbances from eventually steepening into shocks. It closes a gap between the physical expectation and the mathematical theory of nonlinear wave breakdown.

### 3. A microscope maps local quantum coherence onto atom images
Su, Szurek, Douglas, Dag & Greiner use a brief Talbot evolution and phase-controlled lattice recapture to turn off-diagonal correlations—coherence between different lattice sites—into site-resolved atom-density signals. In an erbium Bose–Hubbard simulator, their “quantum coherence microscope” separates superfluid and Mott-insulating regions within one cloud and reads coherence beyond nearest neighbours in a two-leg ladder.

**Why it matters:** Quantum gas microscopes usually reveal where atoms are, while local phase relationships are harder to access. This protocol adds a spatially resolved coherence observable for testing strongly correlated matter and quantum simulations.

### 4. Thin-film lithium niobate makes quantum pulse gating far more efficient
Babel, Alarcón, Serino and collaborators demonstrate a chip-scale quantum pulse gate that selectively converts temporal modes of light. They report 96.8 ± 1.7% mode selectivity, 89.6 ± 0.1% internal conversion at 20 mW, and a normalized conversion efficiency roughly 1,000 times higher than earlier quantum pulse gates. Operation near one input photon per pulse retains 91.7 ± 3.1% measured selectivity.

**Why it matters:** Temporal-mode selection underpins high-dimensional quantum communication, tomography and programmable photonic networks. A large efficiency gain at accessible pump powers improves the prospects for integrated implementations.

### 5. A 1989 monochromatic-path conjecture fails by a superlinear margin
Li & Wang disprove Gyárfás’s conjecture that the vertices of every complete graph whose edges have `r` colours can be partitioned into at most `r` monochromatic paths. For all sufficiently large `r`, their construction requires at least `(1-o(1)) r log log r` paths. They also derive an `Ω(r log r)` lower bound in the balanced bipartite case, disproving a related conjecture.

**Why it matters:** This is not a narrow counterexample: it changes the asymptotic scale of the covering problem and supplies a new baseline for extremal graph theory.

## Watchlist

- **Decoupling translation-invariant topological CSS codes** — Wang, Ni, He, Liu & Gu prove that anyon permutation is the only translation-symmetry obstruction to reducing this family of two-dimensional quantum codes to toric codes and product states, and give a constructive algorithm. Promising for code structure, but still an early preprint.
- **Maximum algebraic connectivity at `2n−4` edges** — Cioabă, Jayarajan, Kannan & Roy prove Kolokolnikov’s 2015 conjecture that the maximum is 2 for every `n ≥ 3`. The submission labels itself preliminary, and another paper on the same conjecture appeared in the release.
