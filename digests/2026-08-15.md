# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-15

Saturday’s primary window produced three strong 14 August journal papers in quantum materials and quantum optics. Because arXiv recorded no 14 August physics or mathematics submissions, the mathematics scan was widened to the past week and contributes one clearly labelled preprint.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Layer number and electric fields program quantum Hall topology
Chen, Liu, Hua and collaborators report quantum anomalous Hall states in twisted rhombohedral graphene whose Chern number — an integer describing the state’s topology — follows the number of layers: devices with three, four and five layers show C = 3, 4 and 5. Electric fields or doping can reverse C or switch a device between C = 3 and C = 4.

**Why it matters:** A normally fixed topological property becomes an engineered, electrically controllable variable, providing a flexible platform for studying correlated states and potential low-dissipation electronic channels.

### 2. A graphene antidot measures fractional quasiparticle charge
Di Luca, Hajigeorgiou, Zhou and collaborators create a gate-defined “antidot”, a small potential hill surrounded by quantum Hall edge channels, in bilayer graphene. Conductance oscillations let them directly extract fractional tunnelling charges, including e/3 at filling factors 4/3 and 7/3; other states behave differently because their edge modes re-equilibrate.

**Why it matters:** This tunable fractional coulombmeter can identify anyonic excitations without some of the bulk–edge ambiguities that complicate interferometers, supporting future studies of more exotic quantum Hall states.

### 3. Filtering removes multi-photon errors from a single-photon source
Sbresny, Calcagno, Kim and collaborators find that an unwanted photon emitted during a short drive pulse has a broader spectrum than the desired photon emitted afterward. A narrowband filter can reject that error component while retaining the target emission. In a quantum-dot cascade, the measured zero-delay two-photon correlation fell by almost an order of magnitude to (7.5 ± 2.0) × 10⁻⁵.

**Why it matters:** Multi-photon contamination causes errors in quantum communication and photonic quantum computing. Coordinating pulse duration and filter bandwidth offers a practical route to cleaner single-photon streams.

### 4. Cyclotomic conjecture proof rules out almost Moore digraphs
Kaur and Kumar’s 10 August preprint claims a proof of Gimbert’s cyclotomic conjecture, an irreducibility statement for a family built from cyclotomic polynomials. A previously established implication then rules out almost Moore digraphs — directed networks one vertex below the degree–diameter limit — for every nontrivial out-degree d > 1 and diameter k > 2.

**Why it matters:** If the proof survives review, it settles an existence question across the remaining parameter range of the directed degree–diameter problem.

- **Stacking-engineered superconductivity in 3R-NbSe₂ (PRL accepted 12 Aug)** — a non-centrosymmetric stacking phase retains Ising-type protection beyond the Pauli limit and enhances nonlinear responses; publication and follow-up measurements will clarify the platform’s reach.
