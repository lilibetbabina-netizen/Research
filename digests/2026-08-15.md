# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-15

Saturday’s notes (Athens) centre on 14 Aug VoRs: Nature Physics turns a bilayer-graphene quantum Hall antidot into a fractional-charge coulombmeter, Nature Materials engineers and electrically tunes Chern numbers in twisted rhombohedral graphene, and Nature Communications both suppresses multi-photon errors with spectral filtering and links a polar surface phonon in KTaO₃ to orientation-dependent interface superconductivity. Friday’s maths arXiv listings supply a sharp ellipsoid-fitting threshold and an explicit counterexample to Zhao’s vanishing conjecture.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Quantum Hall antidot as a fractional coulombmeter
Di Luca, Hajigeorgiou, Zhou, Simon and collaborators (Nature Physics, 14 Aug) define a bilayer-graphene antidot electrostatically and run it in the Coulomb-dominated regime. From the periods of conductance oscillations in gate voltage and magnetic field they extract the charge of tunnelling quasiparticles, including e/3 at several fractional fillings, and argue that hole-conjugate states can show different effective charges because of edge re-equilibration tied to the parity of downstream integer modes.

**Why it matters:** Direct charge readout complements interferometric anyon experiments and offers a tunable van der Waals platform for fractional quasiparticle metrology.

### 2. Layer-engineered quantum anomalous Hall effect
Chen, Liu, Hua and co-authors (Nature Materials, 14 Aug) report QAH states in twisted monolayer–multilayer (1+N) rhombohedral graphene with Chern number C = N for N = 3, 4, 5. Gate doping or displacement field can flip the sign of C in a (1+3) device, and a (2+4) stack shows a displacement-driven transition between C = 3 and C = 4 QAH states.

**Why it matters:** The topological invariant becomes a layer- and electrically programmable knob rather than a fixed material property.

### 3. Spectral filtering of multi-photon errors
Sbresny, Calcagno, Kim, Finley and collaborators (Nature Communications, 14 Aug) show that when a quantum emitter is driven by pulses shorter than its lifetime, the unwanted multi-photon components are spectrally broader than the desired single photons. Narrowband filtering removes most of that prompt error channel and reduces g⁽²⁾(0) by nearly an order of magnitude while preserving useful single-photon transmission.

**Why it matters:** Re-excitation during the drive had looked like a fundamental purity floor; a passive filter pushes below it for photonic quantum tech.

### 4. Surface-phonon polarity and KTaO₃ interface Tc
Liu, Li, Zhang, Cheng and co-authors (Nature Communications, 14 Aug) resolve a ~107 meV anisotropic surface phonon at KTaO₃ interfaces whose polarity scales (111) > (110) ≫ (001), matching the orientation trend of superconducting Tc and enhanced by tens of times relative to non-superconducting interfaces. A softened ~100 meV mode is tied to interfacial oxygen vacancies.

**Why it matters:** Gives a concrete lattice signature for the puzzling orientation dependence of KTO interface superconductivity.

### 5. Sharp phase transition for ellipsoid fitting
de la Cerda, Potechin, Tulsiani and Xu (arXiv:2608.12415) prove that m random Gaussian points in dimension d lie on a centered ellipsoid with high probability below m ≈ d²/4 and fail above that threshold (up to vanishing factors), confirming the Saunderson–Chandrasekaran–Parrilo–Willsky conjecture’s sharp transition.

**Why it matters:** Settles a benchmark phase transition in high-dimensional convex geometry (preprint).

### 6. Counterexample to Zhao’s vanishing conjecture
Prellberg (arXiv:2608.12543) uses a collision-hull compression of homogeneous Keller maps to build an explicit 40-variable homogeneous quartic for which Zhao’s vanishing conjecture fails, after recovering Thompson’s classic 24→20 compression from the same principle.

**Why it matters:** Moves a Jacobian-adjacent algebraic conjecture from open to “understand the counterexamples” (preprint).
