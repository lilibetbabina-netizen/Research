# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-14

Friday’s notes pull from 13–14 Aug VoRs: Nature Physics reports diamond melting under terapascal shocks with the diamond lattice surviving to 1 TPa, Nature Communications finds a Mott gap at the Dirac point in slow moiré Dirac bands of twisted MoSe₂ and uses giant terahertz photoresistance to show electron–electron scattering limits twisted-bilayer-graphene conductivity, while two 13 Aug maths preprints claim completions of Banach’s isometric conjecture (odd dimensions) and Gromov’s 1986 volume growth conjecture.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Diamond melting under shock compression at 1 TPa
Millot, Coppari, Lazicki, Eggert and collaborators (Nature Physics, 13 Aug) shock-compress microcrystalline diamond with velocimetry, pyrometry and X-ray diffraction. The diamond structure persists to 1 TPa — contradicting a prior report of a BC8 transition that DFT expects near that pressure — and the data show shock melting near ~7,300 K with a slightly negative melt slope, aligning experiment with quantum simulations.

**Why it matters:** Carbon’s terapascal phase diagram constrains ice-giant interiors and high-density-carbon ablators for inertial confinement fusion; closing the melt-curve gap and ruling out an early BC8 phase on these paths sharpens both.

### 2. Mott gap at the Dirac point in slow moiré Dirac fermions
Yang, Liang, Hu, Franz, Ye and co-authors (Nature Communications, 14 Aug) build a honeycomb moiré in twisted MoSe₂ homobilayers whose graphene-like bands have Fermi velocity reduced by nearly two orders of magnitude. In this strongly correlated, SU(2)-symmetric Dirac regime they detect a Mott gap at the Dirac point that survives to 110 K, plus correlated states at filling ν = −1 and at fractional fillings.

**Why it matters:** Graphene itself never showed a Dirac-point Mott insulator because interactions are weak relative to the Fermi velocity; a tunable TMD moiré platform makes that regime accessible.

### 3. Interaction-limited conductivity in twisted bilayer graphene
Shilov, Elesin, Kravtsov, Ganichev and collaborators (Nature Communications, 13 Aug) heat electrons with terahertz pulses while keeping the lattice cold. Giant THz photoresistance (several kΩ) shows electron–electron scattering remains important even where transport was attributed to phonons — including linear-in-T resistivity near the magic angle — possibly via inter-valley collisions allowed by broken Galilean invariance in the Dirac dispersion.

**Why it matters:** Hot-electron THz transport separates electron–electron from phonon channels and tests competing pictures of strange metallicity in magic-angle graphene.

### 4. Completing Banach’s isometric conjecture in the real case
Lu and Yang (arXiv:2608.13536, 13 Aug) prove that for every odd n, a real Banach space whose n-dimensional subspaces are all isometric must be Hilbert. With Gromov’s even-n theorem this completes Banach’s 1932 conjecture over the reals (bundle topology + Brouwer degree). An earlier Dec 2025 preprint by Ning Zhang also claims a full finite-dimensional solution; the community will need to compare proofs.

**Why it matters:** The conjecture is a classical rigidity problem linking local subspace geometry to Hilbert structure (preprint).

### 5. Affirmative answer to Gromov’s volume growth conjecture
Ge (arXiv:2608.13553, 13 Aug) proves that complete noncompact n-manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature and scalar curvature ≥ 1 obey Vol(B(p,R)) ≤ C_n R^{n−2}, using the heat-kernel Fisher metric and Nash entropy.

**Why it matters:** Lower scalar-curvature bounds are tied to polynomial volume growth under Ricci ≥ 0 (preprint).
