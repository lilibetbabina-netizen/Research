# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-09

Sunday’s notes center on an 8 Aug Nature Communications pair that finally looks inside magic-angle graphene’s correlated topology — atomic-scale Hall wavefunctions and entropy plateaus of a graphene heavy-fermion state — together with a 7 Aug PRL that hears individual gas molecules hit a levitated sensor, an 8 Aug Optica demonstration of composable multi-user continuous-variable QKD, and a 6 Aug combinatorics preprint that resolves Andersen’s rainbow-path conjecture for large graphs.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Atomically resolved wavefunctions of integer and fractional quantum anomalous Hall states
Han, Jiang, Mao and colleagues (Nature Communications, 8 Aug) park a scanning tunneling microscope on hBN-aligned magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene and image the correlated insulating states at fillings ν = +2 and +3. The ν = +3 insulator evolves into a quantum anomalous Hall state with Chern number C = +1; atomic-resolution maps show sublattice imbalance inside AA regions — direct evidence of valley polarization — with magnetic hysteresis pointing to orbital magnetism. At higher fields, further Chern and fractional Chern states appear, each with a spatial sublattice pattern tied to the occupied valley.

**Why it matters:** For years, moiré topology was inferred from transport. Seeing which sublattice and valley carry the Chern order turns those phases into objects one can inspect, compare to theory, and eventually engineer.

### 2. Entropy plateaus reveal topological heavy-fermion physics in magic-angle graphene
Zhang, Wu, Călugăru, Bernevig, Andrei and co-authors (Nature Communications, 8 Aug) measure inverse compressibility and entropy with planar tunneling spectroscopy in the same magic-angle platform. Mass renormalization tracks filling in a way that matches hybridization between localized and itinerant electrons — the Kondo-lattice language of heavy-fermion metals. Entropy shows two plateaus whose degeneracies encode local-moment combinatorics, including an 8-to-4-fold drop read as strain-driven symmetry breaking.

**Why it matters:** Heavy-fermion physics used to require rare-earth crystals. Putting its entropic fingerprints in a gate-tunable graphene stack makes mass enhancement and local-moment counting into tools for 2D quantum materials.

### 3. Optomechanical sensors detect individual gas-molecule collisions
Tseng, Moore, Carney and collaborators (Physical Review Letters, 7 Aug) watch an optically levitated nanoparticle recoil from single collisions with Kr, Xe, and SF₆. Collision rates recover the gases’ partial pressures; the frequency content of each impulse reports on the particle’s surface and temperature; reconstructed momentum transfers reach about 200 keV/c.

**Why it matters:** Counting molecules one by one offers a path to a primary pressure standard and brings levitated sensors into the impulse range needed for precision tests of rare or weakly interacting particles.

### 4. Composable continuous-variable multi-user quantum key distribution
Kanitschar, Hajomer, Gehring, Pacher and colleagues (Optica, 8 Aug) extend discrete-modulated continuous-variable QKD from two parties to many users sharing a passive optical network. They prove composable finite-size rates for lossy and noisy channels, then run the protocol over 10 km access links, achieving 2.185×10⁻³ secure bits per symbol (0.273 Mbit/s) against i.i.d. collective attacks.

**Why it matters:** Real quantum networks need multipoint keys on telecom fiber, not only lab point-to-point links. A measured, composably secure CV access-network rate is a practical step in that direction.

### 5. Andersen’s rainbow-path conjecture for large complete graphs
Bowtell, Montgomery, Müyesser and Pokrovskiy (arXiv:2608.06369, 6 Aug) prove that every properly edge-coloured complete graph on sufficiently many vertices contains a rainbow path that visits all but one vertex. That settles Andersen’s 1989 conjecture for large n, improving earlier approximate constructions. The same toolkit confirms, for large n, that every Latin square has a cycle-free transversal of order n−2 (Gyárfás–Sárközy, 2014).

**Why it matters:** Rainbow paths and Latin-square transversals are classical existence problems at the border of graph theory and combinatorial design; a large-n resolution clears a decades-old conjecture and links the two settings (preprint).

- **On-chip TFLN polarization Bell pairs (Optica, 8 Aug)** — Kim et al. report a compact continuous-wave-pumped thin-film lithium niobate source of polarization-entangled photons.
- **Pair-breaking models in LaBi₂ films (PRL, 7 Aug)** — Dorrian, Falson et al. argue that multi-channel Kharitonov–Feigel’man analysis, not single-mechanism KLB fits, is needed before claiming unconventional pairing from critical fields.
- **Molecular-hydrogen-ion metrology via Rydberg–Stark states (PRL, 7 Aug)** — Doran, Merkt et al. use high-n autoionizing Stark states for precision fine and hyperfine spectroscopy of H₂⁺ cores.
