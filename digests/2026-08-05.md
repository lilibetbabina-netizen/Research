# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-05

Wednesday’s notes draw on a dense 3–4 Aug peer-reviewed cluster: reactor antineutrinos after shutdown, three-parton structure inside the proton, plasma and ion acceleration, and phononic frequency routing — plus a 4 Aug graph-theory preprint that settles Bollobás’s asymptotic Moore-bound conjecture.

## Top discoveries

### 1. First measurement of residual neutrinos from spent nuclear fuel
The Double Chooz Collaboration (Physical Review Letters, 4 Aug) records the faint antineutrino glow that remains after a reactor shuts down. Over 17.2 reactor-off days, a detector 400 m from the Chooz B cores saw 106 ± 18 candidate events (5.9σ) in the most sensitive window, matching simulations of long-lived fission products in the cores and spent-fuel pools (88 ± 7 predicted).

**Why it matters:** Safeguards and monitoring have mainly watched operating reactors. A validated residual flux means detectors can also check shutdown status and spent-fuel inventories.

### 2. Quark–gluon–quark interference measured inside the proton
Vladimirov, Portela and Rodini (Physical Review Letters, 3 Aug) extract twist-three parton distributions — functions that encode quantum interference among quark–gluon–quark states — from a global fit to 243 data points spanning collinear and transverse-momentum–dependent observables. The distributions show clear flavour patterns and differ from zero at about 2–3σ.

**Why it matters:** Standard parton densities miss these genuine three-body correlations. Mapping them sharpens the proton’s quantum structure ahead of Electron–Ion Collider precision.

### 3. Laser–plasma Raman amplification of an ultrabroadband pulse to 0.3 TW
Shaw, Ambat, McMillen and colleagues (Nature Photonics, 3 Aug) use a laser–plasma Raman amplifier on an ultrabroadband (>60 nm) seed. They reach seed intensities up to 3.7 × 10¹⁵ W/cm², efficiencies up to 8.7%, roughly 30× energy gain from a 7.6 mJ seed, and final powers up to 0.3 TW with pulses as short as 64 fs — about ten times prior plasma-amplifier results.

**Why it matters:** Plasma stages tolerate far higher fluence than glass final amplifiers. That is a practical route toward intensities needed for strong-field quantum electrodynamics on existing petawatt lasers.

### 4. Bow-wake acceleration of protons toward a relativistic regime
Isayama, Nakahara, Morita and collaborators (Communications Physics, 3 Aug) accelerate protons to as much as 60 MeV in dense foam targets via a bow wake at the laser front. Particle-in-cell simulations back the picture that the slower wake in dense plasma lets heavy ions enter the accelerating field.

**Why it matters:** Ion wakefields have been hard because ions are heavy. Dense-plasma bow-wake injection is a laboratory path toward relativistic ions and a controlled analogue of astrophysical wakes.

### 5. Asymptotically attaining the Moore bound
Cames van Batenburg and Korsky (arXiv:2608.03965, 4 Aug) prove that for every fixed diameter k, the largest graph of maximum degree d and diameter k has order (1+o(1)) d^k as d → ∞ — matching the Moore bound asymptotically and proving a conjecture of Bollobás. Their constructions use regular graphs on partial flags over finite fields.

**Why it matters:** The Moore bound is the classical ceiling on dense, short-path networks. Showing it is asymptotically sharp for every fixed diameter closes a central question in extremal graph theory (preprint).

## Watchlist

- **On-chip phononic rainbow capture (PRL, 3 Aug)** — Zheng et al. spatially sort elastic Landau levels and capture them with topological edge states; strong device physics, slightly narrower human-forward scope than the top five.
- **Unitary encoder for surface codes (npj Quantum Information, 4 Aug)** — Tsai & Puri halve the depth of prior unitary surface-code encoders via rotated/regular code conversion; useful on nonlocal platforms, fault distance stays fixed.
- **SVP in 2^{0.6039n} time via mid-point Hessian (arXiv:2608.02478, 3 Aug)** — Hhan’s classical/quantum lattice algorithms improve on the prior 2^{n+o(n)} ADRS baseline; crypto-relevant, still a preprint.
