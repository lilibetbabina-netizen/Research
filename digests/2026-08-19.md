# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-19

Wednesday’s Athens notes centre on 17–18 Aug VoRs after the 17 Aug digest: Nature Communications reports non-volatile gate memory of KTaO₃-interface superconductivity that optical light erases, Nature Electronics unveils atomically thin amorphous carbon with k ≈ 1.35, Physical Review X shows Coulomb screening extinguishing magic-angle graphene superconductivity, Physical Review Letters delivers IceCube’s three-flavor cosmic-neutrino mix and a chiral Little–Parks signature in β-Bi₂Pd, and an 18 Aug maths preprint overturns Chern’s constant-length second-fundamental-form conjecture above dimension two.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Gate memory of KTaO₃ interface superconductivity
Chen, Ran, Dong and collaborators (Nature Communications, 18 Aug) cycle electrostatic gates on AlOₓ/KTaO₃ heterostructures and find that gating promotes and imprints interfacial superconductivity that persists without continuous bias. Cryogenic illumination erases the superconducting state. They link the memory and erasure to polar-nanoregion reorientation and oxygen-vacancy charge traps in KTaO₃’s quantum-paraelectric lattice.

**Why it matters:** A dissipationless superconducting channel that can be written electrically and erased optically is a concrete route to oxide interfaces that combine memory with quantum transport.

### 2. Ultralow-k amorphous carbon at atomic thickness
Toh, Grebenko, Karadeniz and collaborators (Nature Electronics, 18 Aug) grow conformal amorphous carbon films below 300 °C with dielectric constant k ≈ 1.35 down to ~0.8 nm, dielectric strength 28–31 MV cm⁻¹, and a projected metal-ion diffusion barrier lifetime ~10¹⁰ s at monolayer thickness.

**Why it matters:** Conventional porous low-k dielectrics break down as interconnect spacing shrinks below 10 nm; a mechanically robust, directly grown ULK film targets that CMOS scaling wall.

### 3. Screening kills magic-angle graphene superconductivity
Barrier, Peng, Xu and collaborators (Physical Review X, 17 Aug) place two twisted graphene bilayers in immediate proximity while keeping them electronically decoupled. Doping one bilayer screens Coulomb interactions in the adjacent magic-angle bilayer and completely suppresses both superconductivity and the correlated insulator.

**Why it matters:** Earlier null screening tests left room for phonon-mediated pairing; a clean screening kill of Tc and the insulator strongly favours an unconventional, interaction-driven mechanism.

### 4. IceCube’s three-flavor cosmic neutrinos
Using 11.4 years of IceCube data from 5 TeV to 10 PeV (Physical Review Letters, 18 Aug), the collaboration classifies events into three flavor-dependent morphologies and finds a best-fit ratio f_e : f_μ : f_τ = 0.30 : 0.37 : 0.33, consistent with standard oscillations. Source-flavor inference rules out pure neutron-decay production outside the 99% confidence interval.

**Why it matters:** Cosmic baselines test neutrino mixing where accelerators cannot, and constrain how astrophysical sources make high-energy neutrinos.

### 5. Chiral Little–Parks signature in β-Bi₂Pd
Xu, Qin, Shen and collaborators (Physical Review Letters, 18 Aug) measure a linear-in-field tilt of the critical temperature in mesoscopic β-Bi₂Pd rings via the Little–Parks effect, which they interpret as orbital angular momentum of a time-reversal-breaking chiral order parameter coupling to the applied field.

**Why it matters:** Chiral superconductivity is scarce and hard to certify; a mesoscopic Tc modulation gives a transferable experimental fingerprint.

### 6. Chern’s sphere conjecture fails above dimension two
Firester and Tsiamis (arXiv:2608.18074, 18 Aug) disprove the Chern–do Carmo–Kobayashi claim that closed minimal immersions into spheres with constant-length second fundamental form must have discrete scalar-curvature values. Counterexamples exist for all n ≥ 3 with m ≥ 4, and for even n ≥ 4 with m ≥ 3 (the n = 1,2 cases remain intact).

**Why it matters:** A classic rigidity expectation in minimal submanifold geometry does not survive in higher dimensions (preprint).
