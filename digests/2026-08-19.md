# Physics & Maths Digest — 2026-08-19

Today’s Athens scan is preprint-led, so every selected result carries medium confidence pending journal review and wider scrutiny. The strongest items combine demonstrated hardware or synthesis with two substantial mathematical claims: faster programmable light for cold atoms, task-specific computation inside a quantum sensor, data-guided expansion of the MXene family, a dimension-independent harmonic-analysis bound, and a proposed completion of a decades-old matrix-classification problem.

## Top discoveries

### 1. Spatial light patterns switch in 11.8 nanoseconds
Deters, Li, Douglas, Greiner and Young demonstrate a dispersive spatial light modulator that turns telecom-frequency components into arbitrary two-dimensional optical patterns. It reaches a rise time of 11.8 ns—more than 84 million frames per second—with 10⁻³-level intensity control across an 83 × 52 beam-waist field of view. The experiment also moves five optical tweezers along arbitrary two-dimensional paths in about 1.2 μs; its programmable Hubbard-model proposals remain numerical.

**Why it matters:** Neutral-atom computers and simulators need fast, precise light patterns to route atoms, address sites and vary interactions. This architecture attacks all three constraints at once, though large-array homogeneity is not yet demonstrated.

### 2. A quantum sensor computes before it reports
Sen, Ouellet and collaborators use one superconducting transmon both to sense magnetic fields and to run a task-specific quantum-signal-processing sequence before measurement. At matched sensing-time budgets, it beats optimized conventional protocols by up to 14.9 percentage points for static-field classification and 20.1 points for oscillating-field amplitude classification. The advantage is task-dependent: the simplest frequency test is slightly worse than the conventional baseline.

**Why it matters:** When an application needs a decision rather than a complete field estimate, processing the relevant feature inside the sensor can use scarce measurements more efficiently.

### 3. Hidden materials data yield 11 new rare-earth MXenes
Shamshirgar, Portugal and colleagues search existing materials repositories and flag 38 compounds that had been synthesized but not recognized as multilayer MXenes. They then recover five MXenes with a self-propagating synthesis completed in minutes without sustained external heating and create 11 previously unexplored rare-earth M₂CT₂ compounds with semiconducting and varied magnetic behaviour.

**Why it matters:** The study shows a practical loop from mining old data to rapid physical synthesis, broadening a two-dimensional materials family relevant to electronics, sensing and energy research.

### 4. Riesz transforms get a bound independent of dimension
Ouyang, Spector and Stockdale prove that the vector Riesz transform on ℝⁿ has a weak-type (1,1) bound with constant at most 2 for every dimension. Their proof uses a new decomposition tied to an obstacle problem for the fractional Laplacian and resolves a question Elias M. Stein posed at the 1986 International Congress of Mathematicians.

**Why it matters:** Dimension-free estimates remain stable as a problem grows more variables. Here they strengthen a foundational tool used across harmonic analysis and partial differential equations.

### 5. Order-six complex Hadamard matrices receive a complete classification
Cárdenes Wuttig and Tindall claim a finite, branch-complete classification of all order-six complex Hadamard matrices—perfectly balanced unitary transformations—up to standard equivalence. The result fills the first unresolved dimension after orders two through five and includes a Lean formalization based on two clearly stated published hypotheses.

**Why it matters:** Six-dimensional Hadamard matrices structure balanced interferometers and mutually unbiased quantum measurements. The classification gives the open dimension-six mutually unbiased bases problem a more complete search space.

## Watchlist

- **Chern’s curvature conjecture fails in high codimension** — Firester and Tsiamis construct minimal submanifolds whose constant curvature-related values are dense in an interval, disproving the conjecture for n ≥ 3, m ≥ 4 and for even n ≥ 4, m ≥ 3. Codimension one and two remain open, and the 18 Aug preprint needs community checking.
- **Scaling the cold-atom light modulator** — The demonstrated device addresses 11 × 52 waists with one 40 GHz modulator; megapixel operation and maintained homogeneity are projections, not yet experimental results.
