## Findings

### 1. Ultracold neutrons do not oscillate into a mirror world
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** PSI’s ultracold-neutron search (PRL, online 28 Jul 2026) stored ~25 billion neutrons across scanned magnetic fields and found no evidence of neutron↔mirror-neutron oscillations, excluding previously claimed anomaly regions to ~99.98% confidence.
- **Why it matters:** Closes a leading dark-matter / neutron-lifetime speculation channel and forces theory toward other explanations of neutron anomalies.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://phys.org/news/2026-07-ultracold-neutrons-dont-mirror-world.html

### 2. Feige’s conjecture proved (δ = 1 case)
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Nie & Wei (arXiv:2607.24528, 27 Jul) give a short proof that for n independent nonnegative unit-mean random variables, P(sum < n+1) ≥ (n/(n+1))^n ≥ 1/e — the sharp Feige bound — via the Vlassis–Thomas merger K_n. The arbitrary-δ conjecture remains open.
- **Why it matters:** Settles a long-standing distribution-free lower-tail inequality used in algorithms, combinatorics, and concentration theory.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24528

### 3. Nanoscale polar textures imaged in quantum-paraelectric SrTiO₃
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Cryogenic STEM (Nature, 22 Jul 2026) directly images polar nanodomains in SrTiO₃: below ~105 K they self-organize periodically, then fragment into uncorrelated clusters once the quantum-paraelectric regime sets in below T_q ≈ 40 K.
- **Why it matters:** Links SrTiO₃’s giant permittivity, near-ferroelectricity, and unconventional superconductivity to real-space polar order/disorder rather than a featureless quantum fluctuation picture.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10823-x

### 4. Materials-boosted D–D fusion yield plateau below 2 keV
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** UC Davis / Berkeley Lab (Nature Communications, published 18 Jul; covered 23 Jul): deuterium-loaded Pd and Ti foils show a non-vanishing fusion-yield plateau below ~2 keV, far above bare-nucleus expectations.
- **Why it matters:** If confirmed and understood, materials degrees of freedom would become a controllable lever on low-energy fusion tunneling — still early and mechanism-uncertain.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74421-1
