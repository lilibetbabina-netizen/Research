Atomic-scale maps connect valley occupation to topological order in magic-angle graphene

What changed
Han, Jiang, Tian et al. used low-temperature, gate-tunable scanning tunnelling microscopy and spectroscopy on hBN-aligned magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene. They resolved Coulomb gaps at fillings ν=+2 and +3, tracked the ν=+3 state into a Chern-number-one quantum anomalous Hall state with magnetic field, and imaged sublattice asymmetry inside AA regions. At higher fields, integer and fractional Chern states showed distinct patterns tied to the occupied valley.
Why it matters
Spatially linking sublattice polarization, valley occupation and topological order gives theory a sharper microscopic target and experiments a local diagnostic for engineering Chern phases.
Key source
Nature Communications · DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76486-4

Entropy plateaus expose local moments and heavy electrons in magic-angle graphene

What changed
Zhang, Wu, Călugăru et al. combined high-resolution planar tunnelling spectroscopy with inverse-compressibility and entropy measurements. Filling-dependent mass enhancement is consistent with localized–itinerant hybridization in a Kondo-lattice description. Two entropy plateaus encode an eightfold-to-fourfold reduction in local-moment degeneracy, consistent with strain-mediated symmetry breaking.
Why it matters
A tunable two-dimensional heavy-fermion platform, with entropy directly counting local-moment states, could make the links among heavy quasiparticles, topology, superconductivity and quantum criticality more controllable.
Key source
Nature Communications · DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76179-y

A breathing kagome crystal combines ferroelectricity, flat-band optics and chiral phonons

What changed
Yu, Sui, Liu et al. combined structural, electrical, optical and theoretical probes of layered Nb3Cl8. They report breathing ferroelectricity associated with alternating triangle distortions and van der Waals gaps; exciton-like optical peaks involving flat bands in thin samples but a semiconductor-like edge in thick samples; strong optical activity; and multiple chiral phonons.
Why it matters
The same exfoliable material joins switchable polarization, geometrically flat electronic bands and handed lattice vibrations, creating a compact testbed for coupled charge, lattice and magnetic physics.
Key source
Nature Communications · DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76538-9

Element-order spectra now classify every finite simple group

What changed
Grechkoseeva, Staroletov & Vasil'ev complete the recognition problem for finite simple groups using only the set of orders of their elements. They identify cases with infinitely many isospectral groups, two exceptional pairs with exactly two possibilities, and show that all other isospectral groups are known almost-simple extensions.
Why it matters
This closes a decades-long classification programme around a deliberately sparse algebraic fingerprint and precisely marks what element-order data can reconstruct.
Key source
arXiv:2608.01754 · preprint

physicsmedium

Quantum rare events acquire a practical large-deviation principle

What changed
Green, Gupta & Premkumar develop a framework for probability tails in open quantum systems. They track a thermal-to-quantum crossover in an anharmonic oscillator and find more generally that dominant rare fluctuations minimize measurement-induced relative entropy. Their stochastic-inflation application yields a stationary long-wavelength density matrix that breaks KMS symmetry and detailed balance.
Why it matters
An entropy-based variational principle could make exponentially costly rare-event probabilities more tractable in open quantum systems and cosmology.
Key source
arXiv:2608.06319 · preprint
