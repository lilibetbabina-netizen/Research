## Findings

### 1. Atomically resolved wavefunctions of integer and fractional quantum anomalous Hall states
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Han, Jiang, Tian, Li, Watanabe, Taniguchi, Zhang, Jiang & Mao (Nature Communications, VoR 8 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76486-4) use low-temperature, gate-tunable scanning tunneling microscopy and spectroscopy on hBN-aligned magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene to visualize correlated and topological phases at atomic scale. At zero magnetic field they resolve Coulomb-gap features at fillings ν = +2 and +3; the ν = +3 state evolves into a quantum anomalous Hall insulator with Chern number C = +1. High-resolution imaging shows sublattice asymmetry inside AA regions (direct evidence of valley polarization) with magnetic hysteresis linked to orbital magnetism, and at higher fields a sequence of Chern and fractional Chern insulating states whose spatial sublattice patterns track the occupied valley.
- **Why it matters:** Transport and optics had inferred moiré topology without seeing the wavefunctions; a real-space map that ties sublattice polarization, valley occupation, and Chern order supplies the missing microscopic ground truth for designing correlated topological devices.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76486-4

### 2. Entropy plateaus reveal topological heavy-fermion physics in magic-angle graphene
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Zhang, Wu, Călugăru, Hu, Taniguchi, Wanatabe, Bernevig & Andrei (Nature Communications, VoR 8 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76179-y) combine high-resolution planar tunneling spectroscopy with inverse-compressibility and entropy measurements in magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene. Inverse compressibility shows strong, filling-dependent mass renormalization consistent with localized–itinerant hybridization in a Kondo-lattice (topological heavy-fermion) picture. Entropy spectroscopy finds two plateau structures whose degeneracies encode the combinatorial structure of local-moment states, including an 8-to-4-fold degeneracy reduction interpreted as strain-mediated symmetry breaking.
- **Why it matters:** Heavy-fermion phenomenology — mass enhancement, local moments, quantum criticality — was long confined to complex f-electron crystals; measuring entropy plateaus and mass renormalization in a gate-tunable graphene stack turns that framework into a designable 2D platform.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76179-y

### 3. Optomechanical sensors detect individual gas-molecule collisions
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Tseng, Hardy, Penny, Lowe, Baeza-Rubio, Carney & Moore (Physical Review Letters, VoR 7 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/xtzh-6t1f; preprint arXiv:2604.18371) detect momentum transfers from individual collisions of Kr, Xe, and SF₆ with an optically levitated nanoparticle. Event rates match expected partial pressures; the spectral shape of each impulse probes the nanoparticle’s surface properties and temperature; reconstructed impulses reach ~200 keV/c.
- **Why it matters:** Resolving single gas impacts converts levitated optomechanics into a candidate primary pressure standard and pushes impulse sensitivity into the regime needed for precision searches for rare particle interactions.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1103/xtzh-6t1f

### 4. Composable continuous-variable multi-user quantum key distribution
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Kanitschar, Hajomer, Hentschel, Gehring & Pacher (Optica, VoR 8 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1364/optica.588301) generalize discrete-modulated continuous-variable QKD from point-to-point to point-to-multipoint operation, with secret-key rates derived for loss-only and noisy channels in both asymptotic and composable finite-size regimes. In a passive optical network with 10 km access links they report a composable secure key rate of 2.185×10⁻³ bits per symbol (0.273 Mbit/s) against independent and identically distributed collective attacks.
- **Why it matters:** Quantum networks need multipoint key distribution that plugs into existing fiber access infrastructure; a composable CV protocol with measured multi-user rates is a concrete step beyond laboratory point-to-point links.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1364/optica.588301

### 5. Andersen’s rainbow-path conjecture for large complete graphs
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Bowtell, Montgomery, Müyesser & Pokrovskiy (arXiv:2608.06369, 6 Aug) prove that for all sufficiently large n, every properly edge-coloured complete graph Kₙ contains a rainbow path on n−1 vertices (each colour used at most once). This resolves Andersen’s 1989 conjecture for large n, improving prior approximate results that only guaranteed rainbow paths of length n−O(n^{1/2} log n). Related methods also confirm, for large n, a 2014 conjecture of Gyárfás and Sárközy that every Latin square of order n contains a cycle-free transversal of order n−2.
- **Why it matters:** Rainbow subgraphs sit at the junction of extremal combinatorics and Latin-square theory; settling Andersen’s conjecture in the large-n regime closes a long-standing existence question and yields a matching Latin-square transversal theorem (preprint).
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06369
