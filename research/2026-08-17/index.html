Giant bulk photovoltaic effect from MoS₂/Ta₂NiSe₅ interfacial symmetry breaking

What changed
Ma, Leng, Peng, Hao, Meng, Liu, Gan, Luo and collaborators (Nature Communications, VoR 15 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76804-w) build a cross-bar MoS2/Ta2NiSe5 van der Waals heterostructure and show a pronounced bulk photovoltaic (BPV) response localized at the overlap region. Vertical stacking lifts MoS2’s inversion centre; the orthogonal geometry separates intralayer from interfacial photoresponse pathways, and spontaneous interfacial charge transfer strengthens the shift-current channel tied to the broken-symmetry interface.
Why it matters
Shift-current photovoltaics need clean symmetry-breaking handles without opaque mixing of mechanisms; an interface-localized BPV in a vdW stack offers a design route for bias-free photocurrent generation in 2-D optoelectronics.
Key source
Nature Communications — Ma et al. (DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76804-w)

Unconventional staircase topological Anderson phase transitions

What changed
Mannaï, Shu, Haddad, Ren, Chen, Sun, Sati and co-authors (Communications Physics, VoR 15 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s42005-026-02809-y) report successive disorder-driven topological transitions in which the topological invariant increases stepwise with disorder and then saturates in a strong-disorder topological Anderson phase that does not revert to a trivial Anderson insulator. They realize the “staircase” experimentally in a one-dimensional topolectrical circuit that emulates single-wall nanotubes.
Why it matters
Conventional wisdom says large disorder kills topology; a robust, disorder-tunable staircase of topological invariants opens “disordertronics” — engineering topology with disorder rather than fighting it.
Key source
Communications Physics — Mannaï et al. (DOI 10.1038/s42005-026-02809-y)

Nonlinear plasma dynamics erase cosmological dark-photon heating bounds

What changed
Hook, Huang & Shalaby (Physical Review Letters, VoR 13 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/98cx-7t43; preprint arXiv:2510.13956) revisit resonant conversion of dark-photon dark matter into Standard Model plasmons in the early universe. Particle-in-cell simulations show that once Langmuir-wave energy approaches the plasma thermal energy, ponderomotive nonlinearities excite higher-k waves and density inhomogeneities that shut off further resonance. Deposited energy saturates near the electron thermal energy — orders of magnitude below cosmological thresholds — weakening prior constraints by factors of ~3×103–107 across ten orders of magnitude in dark-photon mass.
Why it matters
Resonant-conversion heating had closed huge swaths of dark-photon parameter space; invalidating that mechanism reopens experimental search windows for a leading light dark-matter candidate.
Key source
Physical Review Letters — Hook, Huang & Shalaby (DOI 10.1103/98cx-7t43)

X-ray-driven Hanbury Brown–Twiss spectroscopy of scintillators

What changed
Katznelson, Kasten, Tziperman, Shultzman, Bucher and collaborators (Nature Photonics, VoR 13 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41566-026-01981-5) connect classical HBT intensity interferometry to X-ray scintillation. Photon bunching in the scintillation light, quantified via g(2)(τ), yields the emission time and the number of optical photons per incident X-ray — a characterization method they benchmark across a wide range of scintillator materials.
Why it matters
Scintillator timing and light yield set performance in medical imaging, high-energy physics calorimetry and security scanning; an HBT readout supplies those intrinsic figures without relying solely on conventional pulse-shape assumptions.
Key source
Nature Photonics — Katznelson et al. (DOI 10.1038/s41566-026-01981-5)

Focal-mechanism map of Gofar transform-fault segmentation

What changed
Tan, Fan, Shearer, Behn & McGuire (Nature Communications, VoR 15 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76407-5) build a catalog of 2,323 focal mechanisms from a year of ocean-bottom seismometer data on the westernmost Gofar transform fault (East Pacific Rise). Pure strike-slip events are only ~31%; oblique, reverse and normal mechanisms are common and correlate with seismicity-inferred segments, with reverse/oblique-reverse events clustering in creeping rupture barriers that bound magnitude 5.5+ patches.
Why it matters
Why some oceanic transform patches creep while neighbours host characteristic earthquakes has lacked a mechanical map; linking faulting type to barrier vs seismogenic segments constrains how transform faults limit rupture and exchange stress with the ridge system.
Key source
Nature Communications — Tan et al. (DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76407-5)

Distinct distances in ℝ³ at least N2/3−o(1)

What changed
Tidor, Yu & Zakharov (arXiv:2608.14454, 14 Aug) prove that N points in Euclidean three-space determine at least N2/3−o(1) distinct distances, advancing the Erdős distinct-distances problem in ℝ³.
Why it matters
Distinct distances is a flagship problem of combinatorial geometry; a near-N2/3 lower bound in three dimensions is a major quantitative step beyond prior weaker exponents (preprint).
Key source
arXiv:2608.14454
