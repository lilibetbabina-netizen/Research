Layer number and electric fields program quantum anomalous Hall states

What changed
Chen, Liu, Hua and collaborators (Nature Materials, version of record 14 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41563-026-02711-6) demonstrate quantum anomalous Hall states in twisted rhombohedral graphene whose Chern number is set by the multilayer thickness: twisted monolayer–multilayer devices with N = 3, 4, 5 show C = N. Doping or displacement field switches the sign of C in one device and drives a transition between C = 3 and C = 4 in another.
Why it matters
The experiment turns a topological invariant from a fixed material property into a layer-engineered and electrically switchable control variable, creating a test bed for correlated topological phases and possible future low-dissipation electronic channels.

Nature Materials — DOI 10.1038/s41563-026-02711-6

A bilayer-graphene antidot directly reads fractional quasiparticle charge

What changed
Di Luca, Hajigeorgiou, Zhou and collaborators (Nature Physics, version of record 14 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03412-2) build a dual-gate-defined antidot — a tunable potential hill encircled by quantum Hall edge states — in bilayer graphene. Conductance oscillations directly reveal fractional tunnelling charge, including e/3 at filling factors 4/3 and 7/3. Hole-conjugate states such as 2/3 show different charges, attributed to edge re-equilibration and downstream integer edge modes.
Why it matters
A compact, tunable fractional coulombmeter offers a cleaner way to identify and control anyonic excitations than interferometers whose interpretation is often obscured by bulk–edge coupling; the approach could extend to non-abelian states and other van der Waals materials.
Key source
Nature Physics — DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03412-2

Spectral filtering suppresses a fundamental single-photon error channel

What changed
Sbresny, Calcagno, Kim and collaborators (Nature Communications, version of record 14 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76358-x) show that an unwanted photon emitted during a short drive pulse is spectrally broader than the desired lifetime-limited photon. Narrowband filtering rejects much of the multi-photon component while retaining the target emission. In an InGaAs quantum-dot cascade, the zero-delay second-order coherence falls by almost one order of magnitude to g²(0) = (7.5 ± 2.0) × 10⁻⁵.
Why it matters
Multi-photon events create errors in quantum communication and photonic quantum computing. The result provides a comparatively simple, emitter-agnostic route to purer single-photon streams by co-designing the drive pulse and collection filter.
Key source
Nature Communications — DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76358-x

Cyclotomic conjecture proof rules out almost Moore digraphs

What changed
Kaur & Kumar (arXiv:2608.08997, submitted 10 Aug) claim a proof of Gimbert’s 1999 cyclotomic conjecture, which characterizes when Φₙ(1 + x + ⋯ + xᵏ) is reducible over the rationals. Combined with a known graph-theory implication, it rules out almost Moore digraphs for out-degree d > 1 and diameter k > 2.
Why it matters
If verified, one algebraic irreducibility theorem closes a longstanding existence question across the nontrivial parameter range of the directed degree–diameter problem.
Key source
arXiv:2608.08997
