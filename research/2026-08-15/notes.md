## Findings

### 1. Quantum Hall antidot as a fractional coulombmeter
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Di Luca, Hajigeorgiou, Zhou, Lotrič, Feng, Watanabe, Taniguchi, Simon and collaborators (Nature Physics, VoR 14 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03412-2) build a gate-defined bilayer-graphene antidot in the Coulomb-dominated regime and extract quasiparticle charge from density–field oscillation periods. They report direct fractional-charge measurements (including e/3 at fillings such as 4/3 and 7/3) and show that hole-conjugate fillings can yield different effective tunnelling charges tied to the parity of downstream integer edge modes and edge re-equilibration.
- **Why it matters:** Verifying and controlling fractional quasiparticle charge — not only interferometric statistics — is a prerequisite for cleaner anyon experiments; a tunable van der Waals antidot platform extends charge metrology beyond GaAs and toward other topological materials.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-026-03412-2

### 2. Layer-engineered quantum anomalous Hall effect in twisted rhombohedral graphene
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Chen, Liu, Hua, Xiang, Zhou and co-authors (Nature Materials, VoR 14 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41563-026-02711-6) realize QAH states in twisted monolayer–multilayer (1+N) rhombohedral graphene with Chern number C = N for N = 3, 4, 5. In twisted (1+3) they electrically reverse the sign of C via doping or displacement field; in twisted Bernal bilayer–rhombohedral tetralayer they drive a displacement-field transition between QAH states with C = 3 and C = 4.
- **Why it matters:** Programmable Chern number and in situ chirality control turn the QAH effect into an electrically engineerable topological degree of freedom, a step toward on-demand correlated topological states in graphene multilayers.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41563-026-02711-6

### 3. Selective filtering of multi-photon events from a single-photon emitter
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Sbresny, Calcagno, Kim, Boos, Rauhaus, Bopp, Riedl, Finley and collaborators (Nature Communications, VoR 14 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76358-x) show that multi-photon errors from pulse-driven single-photon emitters carry a broader spectro-temporal footprint than the desired single photons when the drive is shorter than the emitter lifetime. Narrowband spectral filtering removes most of that prompt multi-photon component, cutting the measured g⁽²⁾(0) by nearly an order of magnitude without killing the useful single-photon channel.
- **Why it matters:** Spontaneous emission plus re-excitation had been viewed as a hard floor on single-photon purity; a passive spectral filter that beats that floor improves sources for quantum communication and linear-optical computing.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76358-x

### 4. Orientational surface-phonon polarity tracks KTaO₃ interface Tc
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Liu, Li, Zhang, Zhou, Lu, Chen, Liu, Cheng and co-authors (Nature Communications, VoR 14 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76343-4) use surface-specific nonlinear optics to resolve an anisotropic surface phonon near 107 meV at KTaO₃ and KTO-based heterointerfaces. Phonon polarity scales as (111) > (110) ≫ (001), reaching ~35× enhancement over non-superconducting interfaces and tracking the known orientation dependence of superconducting Tc; a softened ~100 meV local mode is attributed to interfacial oxygen vacancies.
- **Why it matters:** Orientation-dependent KTO interface superconductivity has lacked a clear lattice handle; linking a symmetry-breaking surface phonon (and vacancy mode) to Tc anisotropy frames phonon–defect engineering of these oxide interfaces.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76343-4

### 5. Sharp phase transition for ellipsoid fitting
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** de la Cerda, Potechin, Tulsiani & Xu (arXiv:2608.12415, Fri 14 Aug maths listing) resolve the ellipsoid-fitting conjecture of Saunderson, Chandrasekaran, Parrilo and Willsky up to a vanishing factor: for m i.i.d. Gaussian points in dimension d, a centered ellipsoid through all points exists w.h.p. when m ≤ (1−o_d(1))·d²/4 and fails when m ≥ (1+o_d(1))·d²/4, establishing a sharp threshold at d²/4.
- **Why it matters:** Ellipsoid fitting sits at the junction of convex geometry, high-dimensional probability and computational geometry; a sharp threshold settles the conjectured phase transition that organizes when random point sets remain “ellipsoidal” (preprint).
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12415

### 6. Counterexample to Zhao’s vanishing conjecture via Keller-map compression
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Prellberg (arXiv:2608.12543, Fri 14 Aug listing) develops a collision-hull compression for homogeneous Keller maps that canonically recovers the known 24→20 reduction of Thompson’s cubic example, then exhibits an explicit 40-variable homogeneous quartic (350 monomials) with an exact gradient collision over ℚ(i) for which Zhao’s vanishing conjecture fails.
- **Why it matters:** Zhao’s conjecture is a central algebraic test related to the Jacobian conjecture lineage; an explicit counterexample (even without claimed minimality) shifts the problem from open status to understanding the failure’s scope (preprint).
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12543
