## Findings

### 1. Layer number and electric fields program quantum anomalous Hall states
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Chen, Liu, Hua and collaborators (Nature Materials, version of record 14 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41563-026-02711-6) demonstrate quantum anomalous Hall states in twisted rhombohedral graphene whose Chern number is set by the multilayer thickness: twisted monolayer–multilayer devices with N = 3, 4 and 5 layers show C = N. In a monolayer–trilayer device, doping or displacement field switches the sign of C; in a bilayer–tetralayer device, displacement field drives a transition between C = 3 and C = 4.
- **Why it matters:** The experiment turns a topological invariant from a fixed material property into a layer-engineered and electrically switchable control variable, creating a test bed for correlated topological phases and possible future low-dissipation electronic channels.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41563-026-02711-6

### 2. A bilayer-graphene antidot directly reads fractional quasiparticle charge
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Di Luca, Hajigeorgiou, Zhou and collaborators (Nature Physics, version of record 14 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03412-2) build a dual-gate-defined antidot — a tunable potential hill encircled by quantum Hall edge states — in bilayer graphene. From conductance oscillations versus gate voltage and magnetic field, they directly extract fractional tunnelling charge, including e/3 at filling factors 4/3 and 7/3. Hole-conjugate states such as 2/3 show different effective tunnelling charges, which the model attributes to edge re-equilibration and the parity of downstream integer edge modes.
- **Why it matters:** A compact, tunable “fractional coulombmeter” offers a cleaner way to identify and control anyonic excitations than interferometers whose interpretation is often obscured by bulk–edge coupling; the approach could extend to non-abelian states and other van der Waals materials.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-026-03412-2

### 3. Spectral filtering suppresses a fundamental single-photon error channel
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Sbresny, Calcagno, Kim and collaborators (Nature Communications, version of record 14 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76358-x) show that, when a quantum emitter is driven by a pulse shorter than its radiative lifetime, the unwanted photon emitted during the pulse is spectrally broader than the desired lifetime-limited photon. A narrowband filter therefore rejects much of the multi-photon component while retaining the target emission. In an InGaAs quantum-dot cascade, narrowing the filter reduces the measured zero-delay second-order coherence by almost one order of magnitude, reaching g²(0) = (7.5 ± 2.0) × 10⁻⁵.
- **Why it matters:** Multi-photon events create errors in quantum communication and photonic quantum computing. The result provides a comparatively simple, emitter-agnostic route to purer single-photon streams by co-designing the drive pulse and collection filter.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76358-x

### 4. Cyclotomic conjecture proof rules out almost Moore digraphs
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Kaur & Kumar (arXiv:2608.08997, submitted 10 Aug) claim a proof of Gimbert’s 1999 cyclotomic conjecture, which characterizes when Φₙ(1 + x + ⋯ + xᵏ) is reducible over the rationals. Combining that result with a known implication from graph theory, they conclude that no almost Moore digraph — a directed graph just one vertex below the degree–diameter upper bound — exists for out-degree d > 1 and diameter k > 2.
- **Why it matters:** If verified, one algebraic irreducibility theorem closes a longstanding existence question across the nontrivial parameter range of the directed degree–diameter problem.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08997
