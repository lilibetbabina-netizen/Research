## Findings

### 1. A superconducting cuprate survives at the single-CuO₂-plane limit
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Luo, Song, Yu and collaborators (Nature, 12 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10857-1) isolate monolayer Bi₂Sr₂CuO₆₊δ (Bi-2201) containing a single CuO₂ plane and observe superconductivity with an optimal transition temperature only about 10% lower than in thicker material. Fine oxygen control extends the monolayer phase diagram from an insulator through an anomalous metal to a superconductor as temperature approaches zero. Scaling near the superconductor–insulator transition shows a divergent critical exponent, consistent with anomalous critical behaviour rather than a simple conventional transition.
- **Why it matters:** The experiment establishes a minimal, highly tunable cuprate in which one CuO₂ plane retains most of the superconducting scale. It offers a clean platform for separating truly two-dimensional physics from interlayer effects and probing the anomalous metallic state near the quantum transition.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10857-1

### 2. Nickelate superconducting signatures reach 100.5 K under pressure
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Qiu, Chen, Semenok and collaborators (Nature Communications, 12 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76534-z) synthesized the bilayer nickelate series La₃₋ₓNdₓNi₂O₇ up to x = 2.4 and measured it under high pressure. Nd substitution compresses the lattice, strengthens the spin-density-wave transition and shifts the orthorhombic-to-tetragonal structural transition to higher pressure. For x = 2.1 and 2.4, resistive onset occurs near 93 K, the resistance derivative shows a 96–97 K feature, and radio-frequency transmission detects a superconducting signature at 100.5 K for x = 2.1. The study identifies structural and magnetic trends associated with the higher transition temperature; it does not demonstrate ambient-pressure superconductivity.
- **Why it matters:** This pushes the reported transition frontier of bilayer Ruddlesden–Popper nickelates to about 100 K and offers a composition/structure route for tuning their superconductivity. Understanding that mechanism may help turn pressure-dependent nickelates into a more controllable high-temperature-superconductor platform.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76534-z

### 3. NOvA makes its most precise antineutrino–hydrogen cross-section measurement
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** The NOvA Collaboration (arXiv:2608.12293, 12 Aug) isolates 35,509 muon-antineutrino charged-current quasielastic interactions on hydrogen, \(\bar{\nu}_\mu H\to\mu^+n\), from a hydrogen-rich detector exposed to \(1.2\times10^{21}\) protons on target. It reports \(\sigma=0.538\pm0.009\text{ (stat)}\pm0.010\text{ (syst)}\pm0.055\text{ (flux)}\times10^{-38}\,\mathrm{cm}^2\) at an average antineutrino energy of 1.9 GeV. This is the highest-statistics (anti)neutrino–hydrogen sample and the most precise total cross-section measurement of this process reported by the authors; the non-flux uncertainty is more than four times smaller than the flux uncertainty.
- **Why it matters:** Hydrogen provides a free-proton reference without the nuclear effects that complicate heavy-target measurements. A cleaner interaction benchmark can improve flux calibration and neutrino–nucleus models used to extract oscillation parameters, including the neutrino mass ordering and leptonic CP phase, in long-baseline experiments.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12293

### 4. New bounds determine the tenths digit of the Grothendieck constant
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Saha, Li, Xue, Chaudhuri, Klivans, Kothari and Meka (arXiv:2608.11158, 11 Aug; revised 12 Aug) prove \(6\pi/11\le K_G\le \pi/[2\log(1+\sqrt2)]-3.47\times10^{-4}\). The lower bound limits asymptotically optimal Krivine rounding schemes; the upper bound constructs a new asymptotic family of high-dimensional rounding schemes. Together they determine the previously unknown tenths digit of the real Grothendieck constant as 7.
- **Why it matters:** \(K_G\) is a central constant in Banach-space geometry and controls the worst-case loss when semidefinite relaxations are rounded to discrete solutions, connecting functional analysis, approximation algorithms and quantum information.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11158

### 5. Gluck’s 1985 conjecture for finite solvable groups is proved
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Zhang (arXiv:2608.11525, 12 Aug) proves that every finite solvable group \(G\) satisfies \(|G:\mathbf F(G)|\le b(G)^2\), where \(\mathbf F(G)\) is the largest normal nilpotent subgroup and \(b(G)\) is the largest degree of an irreducible complex character. The proof develops orbit bounds for solvable permutation groups and faithful completely reducible modules, overcoming examples that block a simpler large-orbit approach.
- **Why it matters:** The theorem closes a four-decade problem linking the internal structure of finite solvable groups to their representation theory, giving a sharp quantitative bound on how far such a group can be from nilpotent.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11525

### 6. The 196560 auxiliary-function conjecture for the Leech lattice is proved
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Zhang and Yang (arXiv:2608.12094, 12 Aug) construct radial Schwartz functions in 24 dimensions satisfying the sign conditions and exact ratio \((\widehat g(0)-g(0))/g(2)=196560\) conjectured by Cohn and Kumar in 2009. Their construction uses the radial Fourier interpolation basis and the 24-dimensional sphere-packing “magic function”; it also recovers every nontrivial shell coefficient of the Leech lattice. The number 196560 is the count of shortest vectors in the Leech lattice.
- **Why it matters:** The proof completes a missing sharp auxiliary-function construction around one of mathematics’ most exceptional sphere packings and clarifies how Fourier interpolation encodes the full Leech-lattice shell structure.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12094
