## Findings

### 1. Superconducting monolayer cuprate with a single CuO₂ plane
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Luo, Song, Yu, Ma, Cai, Zhong, Chen, Zhao and collaborators (Nature, VoR 12 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10857-1) isolate a single monolayer of Bi₂Sr₂CuO₆₊δ (Bi-2201) — one CuO₂ plane — and show that superconductivity survives with only about a 10% reduction in optimal transition temperature relative to thicker crystals. Controlled oxygenation of the monolayer extends the Bi-2201 phase diagram into previously inaccessible doping regimes, revealing an anomalous metal between insulator and superconductor as temperature tends to zero, together with anomalous scaling and a divergent critical exponent at the superconductor–insulator quantum phase transition.
- **Why it matters:** High-Tc cuprate physics is pushed to the true single-plane limit in a gateable van der Waals geometry, clarifying which ingredients of cuprate superconductivity require only one CuO₂ plane and exposing quantum-critical behaviour between the Mott insulator and the superconductor.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10857-1

### 2. High-temperature superconductivity near 100 K in Nd-substituted La₃Ni₂O₇
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Qiu, Chen, Semenok, Zhong, Zhou, Li, Ma, Huang and co-authors (Nature Communications, VoR 12 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76534-z) synthesize polycrystalline La₃₋ₓNdₓNi₂O₇ with record rare-earth substitution (0 ≤ x ≤ 2.4). Nd doping compresses the lattice, strengthens the spin-density-wave transition, and raises the pressure needed for the orthorhombic–tetragonal transition. Under high pressure, transport shows superconductivity at all doping levels, with onset Tc rising to ~93 K and resistance-derivative signatures near 96–97 K for x = 2.1 and 2.4; radio-frequency transmission detects superconducting signatures up to 100.5 K at x = 2.1 (~33 GPa).
- **Why it matters:** Bilayer Ruddlesden–Popper nickelates are a young high-Tc family; chemical pressure via heavy Nd substitution pushes reported superconducting signatures to the 100 K scale and ties higher Tc to magnetism and structure, sharpening the design space for ambient-pressure nickelate superconductors.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76534-z

### 3. End-to-end quantum thermal and ground-state preparation with one ancilla
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Ding, Zhan, Preskill & Lin (Nature Physics, VoR 12 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03389-y) give quantum algorithms that prepare thermal and ground states by forward evolution under a system–bath Hamiltonian whose bath is a single reusable ancilla qubit. By designing the bath and interaction, they prove that the dynamics’ fixed point approximates the target state and supply mixing-time guarantees that justify end-to-end efficiency for a class of physically relevant Hamiltonians — a setting aimed at early fault-tolerant devices.
- **Why it matters:** State preparation is a bottleneck for quantum simulation of materials and chemistry. Rigorous, ancilla-light thermal/ground-state algorithms with efficiency guarantees reduce a central overhead on near-term fault-tolerant machines.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-026-03389-y

### 4. Nearly dry bridgmanite in Earth’s lower mantle
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Okuda, Ohta, Mohn, Nishihara, Takaichi, Kawaguchi-Imada, Kadobayashi, Hirose and colleagues (Nature Communications, VoR 12 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76621-1) measure electrical conductivity of hydrous (Fe,Al)-bearing bridgmanite matching peridotitic compositions up to 47 GPa and 2210 K. Proton conduction dominates; water raises conductivity by roughly an order of magnitude versus dry samples. Extrapolating to lower-mantle conditions implies less than ~40–70 wt ppm H₂O in bridgmanite near the top and mid-mantle — a nearly dry present-day lower mantle.
- **Why it matters:** Water content of the lower mantle has been experimentally unconstrained. A dry bridgmanite reservoir reshapes models of deep-Earth water storage, recycling, and geochemical evolution.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76621-1

### 5. A proof of Gluck’s conjecture
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Zhang (arXiv:2608.11525, 12 Aug) proves that every finite solvable transitive permutation group P on Ω admits a subset Δ ⊆ Ω with |P : P_Δ| ≥ ν(P_Δ), where ν(H) is the maximum order of a nilpotent subgroup of H, and an analogous statement for faithful completely reducible actions on modules. As a consequence he settles Gluck’s 1985 conjecture: every finite solvable group G satisfies |G : F(G)| ≤ b(G)², with F(G) the Fitting subgroup and b(G) the largest irreducible complex character degree.
- **Why it matters:** Gluck’s inequality has been a central open problem in finite group theory for four decades; a complete proof for solvable groups closes a long-standing gap between character degrees and nilpotent structure (preprint).
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11525
