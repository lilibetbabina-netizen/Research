### 1. Nickelate superconducting signatures reach 100.5 K under pressure
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Qiu, Chen, Semenok and collaborators (Nature Communications, 12 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76534-z) synthesized the bilayer nickelate series La₃₋ₓNdₓNi₂O₇ up to x = 2.4 and measured it under high pressure. Nd substitution compresses the lattice, strengthens the spin-density-wave transition and shifts the orthorhombic-to-tetragonal structural transition to higher pressure. For x = 2.1 and 2.4, resistive onset occurs near 93 K, the resistance derivative shows a 96–97 K feature, and radio-frequency transmission detects a superconducting signature at 100.5 K for x = 2.1. The study identifies structural and magnetic trends associated with the higher transition temperature; it does not demonstrate ambient-pressure superconductivity.
- **Why it matters:** This pushes the reported transition frontier of bilayer Ruddlesden–Popper nickelates to about 100 K and offers a composition/structure route for tuning their superconductivity. Understanding that mechanism may help turn pressure-dependent nickelates into a more controllable high-temperature-superconductor platform.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76534-z

### 2. NOvA makes its most precise antineutrino–hydrogen cross-section measurement
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** The NOvA Collaboration (arXiv:2608.12293, 12 Aug) isolates 35,509 muon-antineutrino charged-current quasielastic interactions on hydrogen, \(\bar{\nu}_\mu H\to\mu^+n\), from a hydrogen-rich detector exposed to \(1.2\times10^{21}\) protons on target. It reports \(\sigma=0.538\pm0.009\text{ (stat)}\pm0.010\text{ (syst)}\pm0.055\text{ (flux)}\times10^{-38}\,\mathrm{cm}^2\) at an average antineutrino energy of 1.9 GeV. This is the highest-statistics (anti)neutrino–hydrogen sample and the most precise total cross-section measurement of this process reported by the authors; the non-flux uncertainty is more than four times smaller than the flux uncertainty.
- **Why it matters:** Hydrogen provides a free-proton reference without the nuclear effects that complicate heavy-target measurements. A cleaner interaction benchmark can improve flux calibration and neutrino–nucleus models used to extract oscillation parameters, including the neutrino mass ordering and leptonic CP phase, in long-baseline experiments.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12293

### 3. Ultracold-neutron storage tightens laboratory limits on a sterile-neutron sector
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Ayres, Berezhiani, Bison and collaborators (arXiv:2608.12173, 12 Aug) find no significant magnetic-field-dependent loss of stored ultracold neutrons while scanning 5–360 μT. Interpreting the null result as a search for ordinary-neutron to mirror-neutron oscillations, they set 95% confidence limits on the oscillation time reaching about 20 s across mass splittings of 0.3–22 peV. In part of that range the laboratory exclusion is stronger than a model-dependent neutron-star-cooling estimate.
- **Why it matters:** The result closes parameter space for a class of mirror-matter dark-sector models using a controlled laboratory test, reducing reliance on astrophysical assumptions. It is a null constraint, not evidence that sterile neutrons exist.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12173

### 4. The 196560 auxiliary-function conjecture for the Leech lattice is proved
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Zhang and Yang (arXiv:2608.12094, 12 Aug) construct radial Schwartz functions in 24 dimensions satisfying the sign conditions and exact ratio \((\widehat g(0)-g(0))/g(2)=196560\) conjectured by Cohn and Kumar in 2009. Their construction uses the radial Fourier interpolation basis and the 24-dimensional sphere-packing “magic function”; it also recovers every nontrivial shell coefficient of the Leech lattice. The number 196560 is the count of shortest vectors in the Leech lattice.
- **Why it matters:** The proof completes a missing sharp auxiliary-function construction around one of mathematics’ most exceptional sphere packings and clarifies how Fourier interpolation encodes the full Leech-lattice shell structure.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12094

### 5. The length-four index conjecture is proved for every modulus coprime to 6
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Li, Yuan, Yuan and Zhang (arXiv:2608.12310, 12 Aug) prove that every minimal zero-sum sequence of length four over the cyclic group \(C_n\) has index one whenever \(\gcd(n,6)=1\). Earlier work had proved the claim only for restricted moduli or sufficiently large \(n\), with the best explicit threshold at \(4.6\times10^{13}\). The new proof uses multiplicative Fourier analysis, first moments of Dirichlet characters and a finite-group support uncertainty inequality to remove the size restriction.
- **Why it matters:** This closes the remaining central length case in the classification of index-one minimal zero-sum sequences over cyclic groups, linking additive combinatorics, finite-group harmonic analysis and factorization theory.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12310

### 6. Additive energy on any strictly convex curve is nearly diagonal
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Cushman, Demeter and Wu (arXiv:2608.12316, 12 Aug) prove that for points \(\gamma(X)=\{(x,f(x)):x\in X\}\) on any strictly convex planar curve, the three-fold additive energy obeys \(J_3(\gamma(X))\ll_\varepsilon |X|^{3+\varepsilon}\). The cubic exponent is essentially optimal because diagonal solutions already contribute order \(|X|^3\). They derive the sumset consequences \(|A-A|\gg_\varepsilon |A|^{5/3-\varepsilon}\) and \(|A+A|\gg_\varepsilon |A|^{8/5-\varepsilon}\) for every finite convex sequence \(A\).
- **Why it matters:** The theorem reaches the natural near-optimal scale under only strict convexity and strengthens the toolkit connecting additive combinatorics, incidence geometry and Fourier decoupling.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12316
