Nickelate superconducting signatures reach 100.5 K under pressure

What changed
Qiu, Chen, Semenok and collaborators (Nature Communications, 12 Aug; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76534-z) synthesized La3−xNdxNi2O7 up to x = 2.4. Under high pressure, x = 2.1 and 2.4 show resistive onset near 93 K and a 96–97 K derivative feature; radio-frequency transmission detects a superconducting signature at 100.5 K for x = 2.1. Nd substitution also changes lattice compression, spin-density-wave order and the pressure of the orthorhombic-to-tetragonal transition. This is not ambient-pressure superconductivity.
Why it matters
The result pushes the reported bilayer-nickelate transition frontier to about 100 K and identifies structural and magnetic trends that may help make the platform more controllable.

Nature Communications · DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76534-z

NOvA’s most precise antineutrino–hydrogen cross section

What changed
The NOvA Collaboration (arXiv:2608.12293, 12 Aug) isolates 35,509 ν̄μH → μ+n interactions from 1.2 × 1021 protons on target. It measures σ = 0.538 ± 0.009 (stat) ± 0.010 (syst) ± 0.055 (flux) × 10−38 cm² at an average energy of 1.9 GeV. The authors report the highest-statistics (anti)neutrino–hydrogen sample and most precise total cross section for this process.
Why it matters
Hydrogen provides a free-proton reference without nuclear smearing, supporting cleaner beam calibration and interaction models for long-baseline neutrino experiments.
Key source
arXiv:2608.12293

Laboratory limits on ordinary–sterile neutron mixing

What changed
Ayres, Berezhiani, Bison and collaborators (arXiv:2608.12173, 12 Aug) find no significant field-dependent loss of stored ultracold neutrons while scanning 5–360 μT. Under a mirror-neutron interpretation, they set 95% confidence limits on oscillation times reaching about 20 s over mass splittings of 0.3–22 peV; part of the exclusion is stronger than a model-dependent neutron-star-cooling estimate.
Why it matters
The controlled null test closes dark-sector parameter space without relying solely on astrophysical assumptions. It is a constraint, not evidence for sterile neutrons.
Key source
arXiv:2608.12173

The 196560 auxiliary-function conjecture for the Leech lattice

What changed
Zhang and Yang (arXiv:2608.12094, 12 Aug) construct radial Schwartz functions in 24 dimensions satisfying the sign conditions and exact ratio (ĝ(0) − g(0))/g(2) = 196560 conjectured by Cohn and Kumar in 2009. Their Fourier-interpolation construction also recovers every nontrivial Leech-lattice shell coefficient.
Why it matters
The preprint completes a missing sharp construction around optimal 24-dimensional sphere packing and clarifies how Fourier interpolation encodes the lattice’s shell structure.
Key source
arXiv:2608.12094

The length-four index conjecture without a size restriction

What changed
Li, Yuan, Yuan and Zhang (arXiv:2608.12310, 12 Aug) prove that every minimal zero-sum sequence of length four over Cn has index one whenever gcd(n,6) = 1. Multiplicative Fourier analysis, first moments of Dirichlet characters and a finite-group support uncertainty inequality remove the earlier restriction to special or sufficiently large moduli.
Why it matters
The preprint closes the remaining central length case in a classification problem linking additive combinatorics, cyclic groups and factorization theory.
Key source
arXiv:2608.12310

Additive energy on any strictly convex curve is nearly diagonal

What changed
Cushman, Demeter and Wu (arXiv:2608.12316, 12 Aug) prove J3(γ(X)) ≪ε |X|3+ε for points on any strictly convex planar curve, essentially matching the unavoidable cubic contribution from diagonal solutions. They derive |A − A| ≫ε |A|5/3−ε and |A + A| ≫ε |A|8/5−ε for finite convex sequences.
Why it matters
The theorem reaches the natural near-optimal scale under only strict convexity and strengthens links between additive combinatorics, incidence geometry and Fourier decoupling.
Key source
arXiv:2608.12316
