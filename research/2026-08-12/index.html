Fermilab tightens the direct muon-EDM limit

What changed
The Muon g−2 Collaboration searched 2.34 billion reconstructed positron decays from 2019–2020 data for the vertical oscillation caused by a permanent muon electric dipole moment. The result is consistent with zero and sets |dμ| < 1.10 × 10−19 e cm at 95% confidence, improving the previous direct bound of 1.8 × 10−19 e cm.
Why it matters
A nonzero EDM would reveal time-reversal and CP violation beyond the Standard Model. This preprint narrows that space with a direct muon measurement.
Key source
arXiv:2608.11124

physicshigh

Two experiments optically expose Wigner-crystal dynamics

What changed
Two independent Nature Physics papers observe Wigner polarons—hybrid excitations formed when excitons couple to collective motion of an electron crystal—in charge-tunable monolayer WSe2 without a magnetic field. Wang, Menzel, Pichler and colleagues resolve spin-linked resonances; Zhang, Gu, Adlong and colleagues distinguish static and dynamical signals, control spins optically and observe optical melting.
Why it matters
Optical readout provides a fast, non-contact route into the internal motion and spin state of strongly correlated electron phases.
Key sources
Nature Physics — spectroscopy · Nature Physics — control

Eleven nodes compress hyperspectral processing

What changed
He, Tu, Wang and collaborators build recursively designed circuits from partially reflective nodes. By reusing transmitted and reflected paths, one 11-node waveguide reaches spectral complexity comparable to at least 100 cascaded Mach–Zehnder stages. Arrays reconstruct spectra at sub-picometre resolution across an 800 nm bandwidth.
Why it matters
Rich spectral responses no longer require proportionally deep cascades, offering a compact route to broadband spectroscopy, sensing and photonic signal processing.
Key source
Nature Communications

The one-half Hölder endpoint in Lions’ problem fails

What changed
Lukas Niebel constructs a uniformly elliptic real scalar diffusion coefficient that is one-half Hölder-continuous in time and arbitrarily close to the heat equation’s constant coefficient, yet a regular forcing produces a solution with no square-integrable time derivative. The counterexample extends to every dimension and bounded domain.
Why it matters
The preprint confirms the Auscher–Egert conjecture: endpoint continuity alone does not ensure maximal L2 regularity for time-dependent parabolic PDEs.
Key source
arXiv:2608.11194

mathsmedium

Quantum-entropy estimation breaks the quadratic barrier

What changed
Minbo Gao and Qisheng Wang give the first estimator that uses o(d2) copies to estimate the von Neumann entropy of an unknown d-dimensional quantum state at fixed error. Its leading sample cost is O(d2 log2log d / log2d).
Why it matters
Entropy quantifies quantum randomness and entanglement and enters Hamiltonian learning and thermal-state preparation; this preprint lowers a longstanding information cost.
Key source
arXiv:2608.11151

physicshigh

Disorder launches waves through synchronized micromotors

What changed
Braun, Poncet, Morin and Bartolo show that thousands of three-dimensional-printed rotary motors spontaneously choose antiferromagnetic order and synchronize their precession. Fixed differences in rotation speed then seed freely propagating phase waves across mismatched coherent regions.
Why it matters
The experiment shows disorder generating organized signal propagation in active matter, with implications for models of ciliary coordination and animate materials.
Key source
Nature Physics

mathsmedium

Two graph-covering conjectures are settled

What changed
Bo Ning proves Kohayakawa’s 1991 conjecture on induced paths in bipartite Kneser graphs, quantitatively giving w(s) ≥ 4s/(2048s5/2). The result also settles the de Caen–Gregory–Pullman conjecture and sharpens clique-cover asymptotics for complements of paths and cycles.
Why it matters
The preprint closes two longstanding extremal graph-theory problems and quantifies efficient clique representations.
Key source
arXiv:2608.11132
