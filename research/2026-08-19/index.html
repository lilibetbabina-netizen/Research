Gate-imprinted memory and light erasure of KTaO₃ interface superconductivity

What changed
Chen, Ran, Dong, Sun, Li, Yao, Liu, Wu, Jiang, Cheng and collaborators (Nature Communications, VoR 18 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76583-4) report two linked effects in AlOx/KTaO3 heterostructures: electrostatic gate cycling promotes and imprints interfacial superconductivity (a non-volatile “gate memory”), while cryogenic optical illumination erases that superconducting state. They attribute the behaviour to coupling between the two-dimensional superconducting interface and lattice excitations — polar-nanoregion reorientation plus charge trapping/detrapping at oxygen vacancies — against KTaO3’s quantum-paraelectric background.
Why it matters
Non-volatile control of a dissipationless superconducting channel is a concrete step toward oxide interfaces that combine memory with quantum transport, without relying solely on continuous gate bias.
Source
doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76583-4

Atomically thin amorphous carbon as an ultralow-k dielectric

What changed
Toh, Grebenko, Karadeniz, Bhat, He, Zhang, Shi, Rogov and collaborators (Nature Electronics, VoR 18 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41928-026-01685-2) grow wafer-scale, conformal amorphous carbon films below 300 °C that remain ultralow-k down to ~0.8 nm thickness. They report a dielectric constant k ≈ 1.35, dielectric strength 28–31 MV cm−1, and a metal-ion diffusion barrier with projected time-to-failure ~1010 s at 0.8 nm — properties aimed at sub-10 nm interconnect spacing where conventional porous low-k films fail.
Why it matters
Parasitic capacitance and diffusion barriers limit CMOS and 2-D-electronics scaling; a mechanically robust, atomically thin ULK dielectric grown directly and conformally addresses a roadmap bottleneck rather than an incremental materials tweak.
Source
doi.org/10.1038/s41928-026-01685-2

Coulomb screening suppresses magic-angle graphene superconductivity

What changed
Barrier, Peng, Xu, De Beule, Fal’ko, Watanabe, Tanigushi & Geim (Physical Review X, VoR 17 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/z9qg-287y; preprint arXiv:2412.01577) stack two twisted graphene bilayers that stay electronically decoupled while sitting in immediate proximity. Raising carrier density in one bilayer screens Coulomb interactions in the adjacent magic-angle bilayer and completely suppresses both its superconductivity and correlated-insulator state — a screening response earlier single-layer experiments had failed to see.
Why it matters
Whether magic-angle pairing is conventional (phonon) or interaction-driven has been unsettled; a clean screening kill of Tc and the correlated insulator strongly favours an unconventional Coulomb-sensitive mechanism.
Source
doi.org/10.1103/z9qg-287y

IceCube measures the three-flavor mix of cosmic neutrinos

What changed
The IceCube Collaboration (Physical Review Letters, VoR 18 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/vntd-m133; preprint arXiv:2510.24957) analyses 11.4 years of all-sky data from 5 TeV to 10 PeV, classifying events into three flavor-dependent morphologies. The best-fit flavor ratio is fe : fμ : fτ = 0.30 : 0.37 : 0.33, consistent with standard three-flavor oscillations; each fraction is constrained >0 at >90% CL under a broken power-law flux. Source-flavor inference excludes pure neutron-decay production outside the 99% confidence interval.
Why it matters
Cosmic neutrinos travel cosmological baselines; measuring all three flavors down to the O(TeV) scale tests oscillation physics in a regime no accelerator can reach and constrains how astrophysical accelerators produce neutrinos.
Source
doi.org/10.1103/vntd-m133

Chiral superconducting signature in mesoscopic β-Bi₂Pd rings

What changed
Xu, Qin, Shen, Huang, Zhou, Wang & Li (Physical Review Letters, VoR 18 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/qv9w-x78v; preprint arXiv:2509.18781) report a linear-in-field modulation of the critical temperature in the Little–Parks effect of mesoscopic β-Bi2Pd superconducting rings. They interpret the modulation as a signature of a time-reversal-breaking chiral order parameter whose Cooper pairs carry nonzero orbital angular momentum that couples to the applied field.
Why it matters
Chiral superconductivity is rare and hard to fingerprint; a Little–Parks Tc tilt offers a concrete mesoscopic test that can be applied to other candidate materials.
Source
doi.org/10.1103/qv9w-x78v

Chern’s constant-length second-fundamental-form conjecture fails in higher dimensions

What changed
Firester & Tsiamis (arXiv:2608.18074, 18 Aug) disprove the Chern–do Carmo–Kobayashi conjecture that the scalar curvature of a closed minimal immersion of an n-manifold into a sphere Sn+m, when the second fundamental form has constant length, must take values in a discrete set. The property holds for n ∈ {1,2} in every codimension; they construct counterexamples for all n ≥ 3 with m ≥ 4, and for even n ≥ 4 with m ≥ 3.
Why it matters
The conjecture was a long-standing rigidity expectation in minimal submanifold geometry; showing it fails systematically above dimension two redraws which discrete-curvature phenomena can be hoped for in spheres (preprint).
Source
arxiv.org/abs/2608.18074
