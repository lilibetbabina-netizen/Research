An 84-megaframe-per-second spatial light modulator for cold atoms

What changed
Deters, Li, Douglas, Greiner & Young (arXiv:2608.18071, 18 Aug) demonstrate a dispersive spatial light modulator that maps telecom-frequency components to two-dimensional positions. The device produces arbitrary patterns with an 11.8 ns rise time (>84 million frames per second), 10−3-level intensity control and an 83 × 52 beam-waist field of view; one 40 GHz modulator addresses 11 × 52 waists. Five optical tweezers move and braid on a 1.2 μs timescale, while the Hubbard-model schemes are numerical.
Why it matters
Fast, precise optical control is a bottleneck for neutral-atom computing and simulation. The architecture could speed routing and enable local, time-dependent lattice control, although large-array homogeneity remains open.

A single superconducting qubit senses and classifies magnetic signals

What changed
Sen, Ouellet, Khan, Wang, Prabhu, Senanian, Banner, Oliver & McMahon (arXiv:2608.17400, 18 Aug) experimentally combine sensing and task-specific computation in one transmon. Against optimized baselines at matched sensing-time budgets, the protocol improves static-field classification by 3.0–14.9 percentage points and AC-amplitude classification by 3.8–20.1 points. Frequency-task gains reach 15.4 points, though the simplest frequency task is 2.8 points worse.
Why it matters
A minimal quantum system can process a signal before readout instead of estimating it in full. That trade-off could save sensing resources when the required output is a decision rather than a complete reconstruction.
Key source
arXiv:2608.17400

Database mining and rapid synthesis expand the MXene family

What changed
Shamshirgar, Portugal and colleagues (arXiv:2608.16644, 17 Aug) identify 38 previously synthesized but unrecognized multilayer-MXene candidates in materials repositories. They recover five MXenes with a self-propagating synthesis that finishes in minutes without sustained external heating, then realize 11 previously unexplored rare-earth M2CT2 compounds. Measurements and calculations indicate semiconducting behaviour and varied magnetic states.
Why it matters
The work links knowledge recovery from old data to rapid synthesis, offering a potentially more efficient route to enlarge useful two-dimensional materials chemistry.
Key source
arXiv:2608.16644

A dimension-free bound settles Stein’s 1986 Riesz-transform problem

What changed
Ouyang, Spector & Stockdale (arXiv:2608.18068, 18 Aug) prove that the full vector Riesz transform on ℝn obeys a weak-type (1,1) inequality with constant at most 2, independent of dimension. Their proof uses a decomposition based on an obstacle problem for the fractional Laplacian and answers a problem Elias M. Stein posed at the 1986 ICM.
Why it matters
Riesz transforms are basic tools of harmonic analysis and PDEs. Uniform high-dimensional control and the new decomposition may carry into other singular-integral problems.
Key source
arXiv:2608.18068

mathsmedium

Claimed complete classification of order-six complex Hadamard matrices

What changed
Cárdenes Wuttig & Tindall (arXiv:2608.18053, 18 Aug) give a finite, branch-complete classification up to standard equivalence. They prove the missing global step in Szöllősi’s dilation method, recover classes outside Karlsson’s family and Tao’s isolated matrix from suitable 3 × 3 corners, and provide a Lean 4 formalization that retains two published results as hypotheses.
Why it matters
These matrices encode balanced six-mode transformations used in quantum measurements and interferometry. A complete order-six map gives the open mutually unbiased bases problem a firmer search space.
Key source
arXiv:2608.18053

mathsmedium

High-codimension counterexamples disprove Chern’s curvature conjecture

What changed
Firester & Tsiamis (arXiv:2608.18074, 18 Aug) construct closed embedded minimal submanifolds of spheres whose constant squared second-fundamental-form values are dense in a bounded interval. This disproves the Chern–do Carmo–Kobayashi discreteness conjecture for every n ≥ 3, m ≥ 4 and for even n ≥ 4, m ≥ 3. Codimensions one and two in dimensions at least three remain open.
Why it matters
Constant curvature data do not force the expected discrete possibilities in high codimension, sharply redrawing a long-running rigidity programme.
Key source
arXiv:2608.18074
