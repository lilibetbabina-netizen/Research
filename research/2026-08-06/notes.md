## Findings

### 1. Vacuum birefringence in polarized X-rays from a radio magnetar
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Stewart, Dinh Thi, Younes, Lower, Baring, Negro et al. (Nature, VoR 5 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10859-z) report phase- and energy-resolved X-ray polarimetry of the radio magnetar 1E 1547.0−5408 with IXPE, NICER and Parkes/Murriyang. Soft-band polarization degrees reach phase-averaged ~65% at 2 keV (falling between 2–4 keV) and nearly 80% at some phases in 2–3 keV, remaining ≳40% through radio-beam crossing. X-ray and radio polarization angles track the rotating-vector model. The signals challenge non-refractive surface-emission models and are naturally explained by vacuum-birefringence-governed magnetospheric propagation — a long-standing QED prediction in >10¹⁴ G fields.
- **Why it matters:** Vacuum birefringence has been a hallmark unconfirmed prediction of strong-field QED; magnetar polarimetry now supplies a concrete astrophysical test of how extreme B-fields reshape light.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10859-z

### 2. Entangling gate for dual-rail erasure qubits
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Mehta, Teoh, Noh, Agrawal et al. / D-Wave Quantum (Nature, VoR 5 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10822-y; print issue 6 Aug) design and demonstrate a ~500 ns Swap–Wait–Swap controlled-Z gate between dual-rail cavity erasure qubits. The gate preserves the erasure-dominated noise hierarchy: ~0.5% erasure per gate, residual Pauli errors below 0.1%, and bit flips at the ~10⁻⁶ level, with surface-code simulations indicating performance past predicted erasure-biased thresholds.
- **Why it matters:** Erasure qubits promise higher QEC thresholds only if two-qubit gates keep the hierarchy intact; a fast, bias-preserving CZ closes a missing hardware link for scalable dual-rail architectures.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10822-y

### 3. Pseudogap observed in a Fermi–Hubbard quantum simulator
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Kendrick, Kale, Gang, Deters, Lebrat, Young & Greiner (Nature, VoR 5 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10875-z) use a colder cold-atom Hubbard simulator to observe a crossover from normal metal to pseudogapped metal. Compressibility develops a maximum at intermediate doping that traces a thermodynamic anomaly line versus interaction strength; lattice-modulation spectra show a loss of low-energy response concentrated at antinodes, from which they construct a pseudogap phase diagram.
- **Why it matters:** The pseudogap is central to cuprate phenomenology but hard to isolate in materials; establishing it in the Hubbard model itself tightens the microscopic link between doped Mott physics and high-Tc precursors.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10875-z

### 4. Ubiquitous Kelvin–Helmholtz instabilities mixing solar photospheric plasma
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Kuridze, Wöger, van Noort, Rempel, Cameron, Rimmele, Solanki et al. (Nature, VoR 5 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10871-3) use NSF’s 4 m Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope to resolve ubiquitous magnetized Kelvin–Helmholtz instabilities at the edges of photospheric magnetic flux concentrations — experimentally confirming a long-standing theoretical prediction. High-resolution simulations reproduce the structures and support a picture of vortex-driven flux braiding below the visible surface.
- **Why it matters:** Small-scale KH mixing and braiding shape how photospheric turbulence injects and dissipates magnetic energy that ultimately heats and structures the solar atmosphere.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10871-3

### 5. Encapsulation epitaxy of air-stable monolayer NbSe₂ for quantum circuits
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Zheng, Zaman, Zhang, Occhialini, Xu et al. (Nature, VoR 5 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10865-1) report an encapsulation-epitaxy growth mode in which graphene or hBN on SiO₂/Si₃N₄ templates large-area (>1 inch), air-stable monolayer NbSe₂ grown at the encapsulation–substrate interface. Graphene/NbSe₂ heterostructures show Tc ≈ 1 K and enhanced charge-density-wave signatures; integrated circuit elements reach kinetic inductance LK ≈ 0.7 nH □⁻¹.
- **Why it matters:** Air-stable wafer-scale 2D superconductors remove a materials bottleneck for compact, high-kinetic-inductance superconducting quantum devices.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10865-1

### 6. The period–index conjecture is false
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Alexander Perry (arXiv:2608.03684, 4 Aug) constructs, for any algebraically closed field of characteristic 0, a smooth projective threefold X and Brauer class α with per(α)=2 and ind(α)=8, so the period–index conjecture fails; for d≥4 the same ideas yield function-field classes with per=2 and ind=2^d. For d=3 the construction works without uncountability, including over algebraic closure of Q.
- **Why it matters:** Period–index bounds are a central organizing conjecture in Brauer-group arithmetic geometry; an explicit Hodge-theoretic counterexample redraws which classes of varieties can still hope for period–index control (preprint).
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03684
