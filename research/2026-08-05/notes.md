## Findings

### 1. First measurement of residual neutrinos from spent nuclear fuel
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** The Double Chooz Collaboration (Physical Review Letters, VoR 4 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/dr26-j19g; arXiv:2510.04869) reports the first quantitative measurement of residual reactor antineutrino emission after shutdown. In 17.2 days of reactor-off data, a detector 400 m from the Chooz B cores recorded 106 ± 18 neutrino candidate events (5.9σ) in the most sensitive energy window, in excellent agreement with simulations predicting 88 ± 7 events from long-lived fission products in the cores and nearby spent-fuel pools.
- **Why it matters:** Reactor monitoring and nuclear safeguards have focused on operating cores; a validated residual flux benchmark shows antineutrino detectors can also probe shutdown status and spent-fuel inventories.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1103/dr26-j19g

### 2. Quark–gluon–quark interference measured inside the proton
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Vladimirov, Portela & Rodini (Physical Review Letters, VoR 3 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/rd63-hdwp; arXiv:2511.04294) perform the first data-driven global extraction of twist-three parton distribution functions that encode quark–gluon–quark interference. Combining 243 points from collinear and TMD-sensitive observables with complete leading-order twist-three QCD evolution, they find flavor-dependent distributions distinct from zero at the 2–3σ level.
- **Why it matters:** Ordinary PDFs miss genuine three-parton correlations; a quantitative map of those correlations tightens the proton’s quantum structure and frames precision targets for the Electron–Ion Collider era.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1103/rd63-hdwp

### 3. Laser–plasma Raman amplification of an ultrabroadband pulse to 0.3 TW
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Shaw, Ambat, McMillen et al. (Nature Photonics, VoR 3 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41566-026-01977-1) demonstrate laser–plasma Raman amplification of an ultrabroadband (>60 nm) seed. They amplify seed intensities up to 3.7 × 10¹⁵ W/cm² at efficiencies up to 8.7%, transfer >220 mJ from a picosecond pump into a 7.6 mJ seed (≈30× energy gain), and measure compressed pulse durations down to 64 fs with final powers up to 0.3 TW — about 10× prior results — via single-shot SPIDER.
- **Why it matters:** Plasma afterburners can bypass damage limits of conventional final amplifiers, a practical step toward intensities needed for strong-field QED on existing petawatt-class facilities.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s41566-026-01977-1

### 4. Bow-wake acceleration of protons toward a relativistic regime
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Isayama, Nakahara, Morita et al. (Communications Physics, VoR 3 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s42005-026-02749-7) experimentally demonstrate bow-wake acceleration of protons in dense resorcinol-formaldehyde foam targets, reaching energies up to 60 MeV. Three-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations support trapping and acceleration by the bow wake at the laser front, where the wake moves slowly enough for heavy ions to enter the field.
- **Why it matters:** Laser-driven ion wakefields have been hard because ions are heavy; dense-plasma bow-wake injection offers a laboratory path toward relativistic ion acceleration and astrophysical wake analogues.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-026-02749-7

### 5. Capturing an elastic-wave rainbow on a phononic chip
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Zheng, Xie, Wen et al. (Physical Review Letters, published 3 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/bkg4-9pr4) realise an on-chip elastic-wave rainbow by engineering pseudomagnetic and pseudoelectric fields so elastic Landau-level modes of different frequencies separate spatially in a silicon phononic crystal. Topological chiral edge states from the pseudomagnetic field capture the bulk rainbow; both the rainbow distribution and its capture are measured directly.
- **Why it matters:** Frequency-sorted elastic modes plus topological routing give a compact architecture for multifrequency integrated acoustics and on-chip signal processing.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1103/bkg4-9pr4

### 6. Asymptotically attaining the Moore bound
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Cames van Batenburg & Korsky (arXiv:2608.03965, 4 Aug) prove that for every fixed diameter k, lim_{d→∞} n_k(d)/d^k = 1, where n_k(d) is the maximum order of a graph with maximum degree ≤ d and diameter ≤ k — resolving the asymptotic degree–diameter problem and a conjecture of Bollobás. The lower bound uses regular graphs H_{k,q} on partial flags in F_q^{2k+1} with diameter k and order (1+o(1))Δ^k; they also give near-optimal constructions for line-graph diameter.
- **Why it matters:** The Moore bound is the classical ceiling on how large a bounded-degree, small-diameter network can be; proving it is asymptotically sharp for every fixed diameter settles a central question in extremal graph theory (preprint).
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03965
