First measurement of residual neutrinos from spent nuclear fuel

What changed
The Double Chooz Collaboration (Physical Review Letters, VoR 4 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/dr26-j19g; arXiv:2510.04869) reports the first quantitative measurement of residual reactor antineutrino emission after shutdown. In 17.2 days of reactor-off data, a detector 400 m from the Chooz B cores recorded 106 ± 18 neutrino candidate events (5.9σ) in the most sensitive energy window, in excellent agreement with simulations predicting 88 ± 7 events from long-lived fission products in the cores and nearby spent-fuel pools.
Why it matters
Reactor monitoring and nuclear safeguards have focused on operating cores; a validated residual flux benchmark shows antineutrino detectors can also probe shutdown status and spent-fuel inventories.
Key source
Physical Review Letters — Double Chooz Collaboration (DOI 10.1103/dr26-j19g)

Quark–gluon–quark interference measured inside the proton

What changed
Vladimirov, Portela & Rodini (Physical Review Letters, VoR 3 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/rd63-hdwp; arXiv:2511.04294) perform the first data-driven global extraction of twist-three parton distribution functions that encode quark–gluon–quark interference. Combining 243 points from collinear and TMD-sensitive observables with complete leading-order twist-three QCD evolution, they find flavor-dependent distributions distinct from zero at the 2–3σ level.
Why it matters
Ordinary PDFs miss genuine three-parton correlations; a quantitative map of those correlations tightens the proton’s quantum structure and frames precision targets for the Electron–Ion Collider era.
Key source
Physical Review Letters — Vladimirov et al. (DOI 10.1103/rd63-hdwp)

Laser–plasma Raman amplification of an ultrabroadband pulse to 0.3 TW

What changed
Shaw, Ambat, McMillen et al. (Nature Photonics, VoR 3 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41566-026-01977-1) demonstrate laser–plasma Raman amplification of an ultrabroadband (>60 nm) seed. They amplify seed intensities up to 3.7 × 10¹⁵ W/cm² at efficiencies up to 8.7%, transfer >220 mJ from a picosecond pump into a 7.6 mJ seed (≈30× energy gain), and measure compressed pulse durations down to 64 fs with final powers up to 0.3 TW — about 10× prior results — via single-shot SPIDER.
Why it matters
Plasma afterburners can bypass damage limits of conventional final amplifiers, a practical step toward intensities needed for strong-field QED on existing petawatt-class facilities.
Key source
Nature Photonics — Shaw et al. (DOI 10.1038/s41566-026-01977-1)

Bow-wake acceleration of protons toward a relativistic regime

What changed
Isayama, Nakahara, Morita et al. (Communications Physics, VoR 3 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s42005-026-02749-7) experimentally demonstrate bow-wake acceleration of protons in dense resorcinol-formaldehyde foam targets, reaching energies up to 60 MeV. Three-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations support trapping and acceleration by the bow wake at the laser front, where the wake moves slowly enough for heavy ions to enter the field.
Why it matters
Laser-driven ion wakefields have been hard because ions are heavy; dense-plasma bow-wake injection offers a laboratory path toward relativistic ion acceleration and astrophysical wake analogues.
Key source
Communications Physics — Isayama et al. (DOI 10.1038/s42005-026-02749-7)

Capturing an elastic-wave rainbow on a phononic chip

What changed
Zheng, Xie, Wen et al. (Physical Review Letters, published 3 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/bkg4-9pr4) realise an on-chip elastic-wave rainbow by engineering pseudomagnetic and pseudoelectric fields so elastic Landau-level modes of different frequencies separate spatially in a silicon phononic crystal. Topological chiral edge states from the pseudomagnetic field capture the bulk rainbow; both the rainbow distribution and its capture are measured directly.
Why it matters
Frequency-sorted elastic modes plus topological routing give a compact architecture for multifrequency integrated acoustics and on-chip signal processing.
Key source
Physical Review Letters — Zheng et al. (DOI 10.1103/bkg4-9pr4)

Asymptotically attaining the Moore bound

What changed
Cames van Batenburg & Korsky (arXiv:2608.03965, 4 Aug) prove that for every fixed diameter k, limd→∞ nk(d)/dk = 1, where nk(d) is the maximum order of a graph with maximum degree ≤ d and diameter ≤ k — resolving the asymptotic degree–diameter problem and a conjecture of Bollobás. The lower bound uses regular graphs Hk,q on partial flags in Fq2k+1 with diameter k and order (1+o(1))Δk; they also give near-optimal constructions for line-graph diameter.
Why it matters
The Moore bound is the classical ceiling on how large a bounded-degree, small-diameter network can be; proving it is asymptotically sharp for every fixed diameter settles a central question in extremal graph theory (preprint).
Key source
arXiv:2608.03965
