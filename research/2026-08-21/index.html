A layered altermagnet shows spin-split bands

What changed
Sprague, Mondal, Sakhya and collaborators identify Co1/4TaSe2 as a layered altermagnetic material. Magnetic susceptibility gives type-A antiferromagnetic order at 178 K, while spin-resolved and conventional photoemission show Fermi-surface spin splitting consistent with density-functional calculations. Heating above the ordering temperature shifts the reconstructed valence bands as the order is suppressed.
Why it matters
A layered compound with experimentally resolved spin splitting and a comparatively high ordering temperature provides a concrete platform for altermagnetic physics and future spin-transport tests.
Source
Nature Communications · DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76784-x

Spatial vacuum fluctuations imaged in an analogue quantum field

What changed
Zhang, Wang, Jiang and collaborators directly image spatial fluctuations of a bosonic quantum field in a planar atomic Bose–Einstein condensate. Two coherently coupled spin components emulate a massive relativistic sine-Gordon field; snapshots contain fluctuations over several length scales with amplitudes consistent with the predicted vacuum state.
Why it matters
Vacuum fluctuations are normally inferred from their consequences. Their spatial statistics can now be examined in a controllable analogue field and used to explore regimes that are difficult to calculate.
Source
arXiv:2608.20311

Phase-stable attosecond soft-X-ray pulse pairs

What changed
Robles, Cesar, Driver and collaborators generate two mutually phase-stable attosecond soft-X-ray pulses at LCLS-II. A split undulator reuses microbunching from the first stage to seed a detuned second pulse; angular streaking measures controllable delays in 250-attosecond steps, while phase shifters tune the relative phase.
Why it matters
Tunable timing and phase at soft-X-ray energies can enable coherent pump–probe control of electronic and molecular motion on its natural timescale.
Source
arXiv:2608.20022

Milnor’s cartography problem solved

What changed
Anton Petrunin proves that among all convex regions of fixed area on the sphere, the round disk requires the greatest distortion when projected onto the plane, resolving a problem posed by John Milnor.
Why it matters
The result identifies the extremal shape in a clean optimization problem connecting spherical geometry with unavoidable map distortion.
Source
arXiv:2608.20265

mathsmedium

Exact asymptotic constant in Schäffer’s matrix inequality

What changed
Houache, Szehr and Zarouf prove Sn/√n → √e for the optimal constant in Schäffer’s determinant–inverse-norm inequality on complex Banach spaces. The result shows that Schäffer’s 1970 upper bound √(en) is asymptotically sharp, including its constant.
Why it matters
This closes a more-than-50-year gap and supplies explicit norms and matrices approaching the extremal behavior.
Source
arXiv:2608.20217
