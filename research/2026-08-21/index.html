Laser Mössbauer spectroscopy of thorium-229 in CaF₂

What changed
Hiraki, Masuda, Takatori, Schaden, Bartokos, Beeks, Fukunaga, Grüneis and collaborators (Science, VoR 20 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1126/science.aea7978) extend classical Mössbauer spectroscopy into the optical range with a vacuum-ultraviolet laser probing the low-energy nuclear isomer transition of 229Th doped in CaF2 crystals. They resolve four distinct thorium doping sites, extract electric-field gradients from the host interaction, identify the microscopic structure of the two dominant configurations, and use site-selective laser excitation to measure isomeric lifetimes and laser-induced quenching at each site.
Why it matters
Solid-state nuclear clocks need precise knowledge of how the 229Th nucleus sits in its crystal host; optical Mössbauer spectroscopy turns that nuclear environment into a directly addressable spectroscopic map.
Source
doi.org/10.1126/science.aea7978

Electron–ytterbium interactions mapped with Rydberg molecules

What changed
Legrand, Wang, Pausewang, Alt, Uruñuela, Hofferberth, Simić, Eiles and collaborators (Physical Review X, VoR 20 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/tjv3-485v; arXiv:2512.20609) measure ultralong-range Rydberg molecules of 174Yb in 6sns 1S0 states across nearly two decades in principal quantum number and three orders of magnitude in binding energy. Coulomb Green’s-function Born–Oppenheimer potentials match high-resolution spectra, yielding electron–Yb scattering phase shifts (including the zero-energy s-wave scattering length and two spin–orbit-split p-wave shape resonances). The data strongly indicate that the Yb− anion exists only as a metastable resonance, and ULRM spectra further constrain an atomic quantum defect.
Why it matters
Ytterbium is a leading divalent platform for ultracold atoms and Rydberg quantum optics; quantitative electron–Yb scattering parameters are foundational inputs that alkalis already enjoy and Yb has lacked.
Source
doi.org/10.1103/tjv3-485v

Heterostructuring as a route to electron-doped nickelate superconductors

What changed
Deng, Kitatani, Jiang, Guo, Witt, Zhang, Wu, Jiang and collaborators (Physical Review Letters, VoR 20 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/qp4p-x6g3; arXiv:2607.08553) propose a disorder-free electron-doping pathway for Ruddlesden–Popper nickelates via first-principles calculations. Intercalating wide-gap insulating LaXO3 (X = Al, Ga, Sc) layers into La2NiO4 lets extra (LaO)+ sheets donate carriers into Ni 3d orbitals, placing La2NiO4:La2AlO4 in the predicted optimal window for dx2−y2-wave superconductivity with Tc exceeding 50 K; the same motif is argued to electron-dope La3Ni2O7.
Why it matters
Electron-doped nickelates have remained experimentally stubborn compared with hole-doped cuprate analogues; a chemically clean heterostructure knob would open the missing half of the nickelate phase diagram (theory).
Source
doi.org/10.1103/qp4p-x6g3

Robust trapped-ion geometric phase gates without ground-state cooling

What changed
Bowers, Palani, Barta, Guglielmo, Libby, Leibfried & Slichter (Physical Review Letters, VoR 20 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/x9b9-5d78; arXiv:2511.14364) generate two-qubit entanglement in trapped ions by adiabatically ramping both the amplitude of a state-dependent force and the motional mode frequencies. The geometric-phase entangling operation remains robust to motional occupation and frequency drifts, with measured Bell-state fidelities above 0.99 across a broad ramp-parameter range and with occupations up to ~10 phonons.
Why it matters
High-fidelity ion gates that do not require ground-state cooling cut calibration and cooling overhead for quantum logic spectroscopy and larger trapped-ion processors.
Source
doi.org/10.1103/x9b9-5d78

Continuum theory for self-dual Higgs transitions beyond the toric code

What changed
Ji, Lanzetta, Zhou & Wang (Physical Review Letters, VoR 20 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/flfk-m8lj; arXiv:2601.20945) propose an SO(4)2,−2 Chern–Simons–Higgs continuum field theory for the enigmatic continuous transition from the self-dual-deformed toric code into a topologically trivial phase that spontaneously breaks the electric–magnetic ℤ2 duality symmetry. The construction generalises to an SO(4)k,−k series describing analogous transitions of non-Abelian topological orders (e.g. double Fibonacci for k=3, S3 quantum double for k=4), and for k=1 they conjecture infrared duality to the 3d Ising transition.
Why it matters
Self-dual continuous transitions have lacked a convincing continuum description; a controlled Chern–Simons–Higgs series would organise a family of duality-preserving topological quantum critical points.
Source
doi.org/10.1103/flfk-m8lj

Micrometer-long sheathed single-metal-atom copper chains under pressure

What changed
Zhang, Dong, Qiu, Yang, Li, Ma, Fei, Zeng and collaborators (Science, VoR 20 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1126/science.aeg0028) synthesise milligram-scale, micrometer-long carbon-sheathed copper single-metal-atom chains by compressing β-copper phthalocyanine above ~21 GPa. The chains are atomically ordered, isolable by acid-assisted exfoliation, stable with a confined Cu–Cu spacing of 2.57 Å, and show signatures of anisotropic conductance and antiferromagnetic interactions in experiment and computation.
Why it matters
True one-atom-wide metallic wires have been hard to make in usable lengths; a pressure route to isolable, sheathed SMACs supplies a concrete platform for one-dimensional electronic and spintronic studies.
Source
doi.org/10.1126/science.aeg0028

Milnor’s cartography problem solved for convex spherical regions

What changed
Anton Petrunin (arXiv:2608.20265, 20 Aug) solves John Milnor’s cartography problem: among all convex regions of a given area on the sphere, the round disk requires the greatest distortion when mapped cartographically onto the plane.
Why it matters
Distortion bounds for map projections are classical geometric questions with a crisp extremal answer; identifying the round disk as the worst case closes a named problem in spherical geometry (preprint).
Source
arxiv.org/abs/2608.20265
