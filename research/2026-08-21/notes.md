## Findings

### 1. Laser Mössbauer spectroscopy of thorium-229 in CaF₂
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Hiraki, Masuda, Takatori, Schaden, Bartokos, Beeks, Fukunaga, Grüneis and collaborators (Science, VoR 20 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1126/science.aea7978) extend classical Mössbauer spectroscopy into the optical range with a vacuum-ultraviolet laser probing the low-energy nuclear isomer transition of ²²⁹Th doped in CaF₂ crystals. They resolve four distinct thorium doping sites, extract electric-field gradients from the host interaction, identify the microscopic structure of the two dominant configurations, and use site-selective laser excitation to measure isomeric lifetimes and laser-induced quenching at each site.
- **Why it matters:** Solid-state nuclear clocks need precise knowledge of how the ²²⁹Th nucleus sits in its crystal host; optical Mössbauer spectroscopy turns that nuclear environment into a directly addressable spectroscopic map.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aea7978

### 2. Electron–ytterbium interactions mapped with Rydberg molecules
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Legrand, Wang, Pausewang, Alt, Uruñuela, Hofferberth, Simić, Eiles and collaborators (Physical Review X, VoR 20 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/tjv3-485v; arXiv:2512.20609) measure ultralong-range Rydberg molecules of ¹⁷⁴Yb in 6sns ¹S₀ states across nearly two decades in principal quantum number and three orders of magnitude in binding energy. Coulomb Green’s-function Born–Oppenheimer potentials match high-resolution spectra, yielding electron–Yb scattering phase shifts (including the zero-energy s-wave scattering length and two spin–orbit-split p-wave shape resonances). The data strongly indicate that the Yb⁻ anion exists only as a metastable resonance, and ULRM spectra further constrain an atomic quantum defect.
- **Why it matters:** Ytterbium is a leading divalent platform for ultracold atoms and Rydberg quantum optics; quantitative electron–Yb scattering parameters are foundational inputs that alkalis already enjoy and Yb has lacked.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1103/tjv3-485v

### 3. Heterostructuring as a route to electron-doped nickelate superconductors
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Deng, Kitatani, Jiang, Guo, Witt, Zhang, Wu, Jiang and collaborators (Physical Review Letters, VoR 20 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/qp4p-x6g3; arXiv:2607.08553) propose a disorder-free electron-doping pathway for Ruddlesden–Popper nickelates via first-principles calculations. Intercalating wide-gap insulating LaXO₃ (X = Al, Ga, Sc) layers into La₂NiO₄ lets extra (LaO)⁺ sheets donate carriers into Ni 3d orbitals, placing La₂NiO₄:La₂AlO₄ in the predicted optimal window for dₓ²−ᵧ²-wave superconductivity with Tc exceeding 50 K; the same motif is argued to electron-dope La₃Ni₂O₇.
- **Why it matters:** Electron-doped nickelates have remained experimentally stubborn compared with hole-doped cuprate analogues; a chemically clean heterostructure knob would open the missing half of the nickelate phase diagram (theory).
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1103/qp4p-x6g3

### 4. Robust trapped-ion geometric phase gates without ground-state cooling
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Bowers, Palani, Barta, Guglielmo, Libby, Leibfried & Slichter (Physical Review Letters, VoR 20 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/x9b9-5d78; arXiv:2511.14364) generate two-qubit entanglement in trapped ions by adiabatically ramping both the amplitude of a state-dependent force and the motional mode frequencies. The geometric-phase entangling operation remains robust to motional occupation and frequency drifts, with measured Bell-state fidelities above 0.99 across a broad ramp-parameter range and with occupations up to ~10 phonons.
- **Why it matters:** High-fidelity ion gates that do not require ground-state cooling cut calibration and cooling overhead for quantum logic spectroscopy and larger trapped-ion processors.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1103/x9b9-5d78

### 5. Continuum theory for self-dual Higgs transitions beyond the toric code
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Ji, Lanzetta, Zhou & Wang (Physical Review Letters, VoR 20 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/flfk-m8lj; arXiv:2601.20945) propose an SO(4)₂,₋₂ Chern–Simons–Higgs continuum field theory for the enigmatic continuous transition from the self-dual-deformed toric code into a topologically trivial phase that spontaneously breaks the electric–magnetic ℤ₂ duality symmetry. The construction generalises to an SO(4)ₖ,₋ₖ series describing analogous transitions of non-Abelian topological orders (e.g. double Fibonacci for k=3, S₃ quantum double for k=4), and for k=1 they conjecture infrared duality to the 3d Ising transition.
- **Why it matters:** Self-dual continuous transitions have lacked a convincing continuum description; a controlled Chern–Simons–Higgs series would organise a family of duality-preserving topological quantum critical points.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1103/flfk-m8lj

### 6. Micrometer-long sheathed single-metal-atom copper chains under pressure
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Zhang, Dong, Qiu, Yang, Li, Ma, Fei, Zeng and collaborators (Science, VoR 20 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1126/science.aeg0028) synthesise milligram-scale, micrometer-long carbon-sheathed copper single-metal-atom chains by compressing β-copper phthalocyanine above ~21 GPa. The chains are atomically ordered, isolable by acid-assisted exfoliation, stable with a confined Cu–Cu spacing of 2.57 Å, and show signatures of anisotropic conductance and antiferromagnetic interactions in experiment and computation.
- **Why it matters:** True one-atom-wide metallic wires have been hard to make in usable lengths; a pressure route to isolable, sheathed SMACs supplies a concrete platform for one-dimensional electronic and spintronic studies.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aeg0028

### 7. Milnor’s cartography problem solved for convex spherical regions
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Anton Petrunin (arXiv:2608.20265, 20 Aug) solves John Milnor’s cartography problem: among all convex regions of a given area on the sphere, the round disk requires the greatest distortion when mapped cartographically onto the plane.
- **Why it matters:** Distortion bounds for map projections are classical geometric questions with a crisp extremal answer; identifying the round disk as the worst case closes a named problem in spherical geometry (preprint).
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20265
