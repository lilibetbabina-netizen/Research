## Findings

### 1. A layered altermagnet shows spin-split bands at the Fermi surface
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Sprague, Mondal, Sakhya, Regmi, Sadhukhan, Kumay, Sheokand, Mazin, Ghimire & Neupane (Nature Communications, published 20 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76784-x) identify Co₁/₄TaSe₂ as a layered altermagnetic material. Magnetic susceptibility gives type-A antiferromagnetic order with a Néel temperature of 178 K, while spin-resolved and conventional angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy show band structure and spin splitting at the Fermi surface consistent with density-functional calculations. Heating above the Néel temperature shifts the reconstructed valence bands as the order is suppressed.
- **Why it matters:** Altermagnets combine zero-net-moment magnetic order with momentum-dependent spin splitting. A layered compound with experimentally resolved splitting and a comparatively high ordering temperature provides a concrete platform for testing the phase and, eventually, spin-transport concepts.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76784-x

### 2. Spatial vacuum fluctuations imaged in an analogue quantum field
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Zhang, Wang, Jiang, Jenkins, Wong, Eigen, Carlse & Hadzibabic (arXiv:2608.20311, submitted 20 Aug) directly image spatial fluctuations of a bosonic quantum field in a homogeneous planar atomic Bose–Einstein condensate. Two coherently coupled spin components emulate a massive relativistic sine-Gordon field; field snapshots contain fluctuations over several length scales, with scale-dependent amplitudes consistent with the predicted vacuum state.
- **Why it matters:** Vacuum fluctuations are usually inferred through effects such as spontaneous emission or the Casimir force. Seeing their spatial statistics in a controllable analogue field creates an experimental route to relativistic-field regimes that can be difficult to calculate directly.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20311

### 3. Phase-stable attosecond soft-X-ray pulse pairs from LCLS-II
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Robles, Cesar, Driver, Franz, Guo, Just, Larsen, Lemons, Lin, Obaid, Sudar, Wang, Zhang, Cryan & Marinelli (arXiv:2608.20022, submitted 20 Aug) generate two mutually phase-stable attosecond soft-X-ray pulses in a cascaded free-electron laser at LCLS-II. A split undulator reuses microbunching from the first stage to seed a detuned second pulse; angular streaking measures controllable delays with 250-attosecond steps, and inter-undulator phase shifters tune the relative phase.
- **Why it matters:** Independently tunable timing and phase at soft-X-ray energies can turn ultrafast measurements into coherent pump–probe control of electronic and molecular motion on its natural timescale.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20022

### 4. Milnor’s cartography problem solved
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Anton Petrunin (arXiv:2608.20265, submitted 20 Aug) proves that among all convex regions of fixed area on the sphere, the round disk requires the greatest distortion when projected onto the plane, resolving a problem posed by John Milnor.
- **Why it matters:** The result identifies the extremal shape in a clean geometric optimization problem connecting spherical geometry with the unavoidable distortion of planar maps.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20265

### 5. The exact asymptotic constant in Schäffer’s matrix inequality
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Houache, Szehr & Zarouf (arXiv:2608.20217, submitted 20 Aug) determine the sharp large-dimension behavior of the smallest constant Sₙ satisfying |det T|‖T⁻¹‖ ≤ Sₙ‖T‖ⁿ⁻¹ for invertible operators on every n-dimensional complex Banach space. They prove Sₙ/√n → √e, showing that Schäffer’s 1970 upper bound √(en) is asymptotically sharp, including its constant.
- **Why it matters:** This closes a more-than-50-year gap between upper and lower bounds and gives explicit Banach-space norms and matrices that approach the extremal behavior.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20217
