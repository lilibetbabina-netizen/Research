## Findings

### 1. Correlated electrons push high-harmonic X-rays past the single-electron cutoff
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Wang, Yan, de las Heras, Song, Prodanov, Wu, Plaja, Popmintchev & Popmintchev (Nature Photonics, VoR 7 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41566-026-01976-2) report a UV-driven high-harmonic spectrum in helium with a weak secondary plateau that extends the conventional single-active-electron cutoff beyond ~120 eV up to the water window at ~280 eV. They attribute the feature to double-electron recombination (DER) of strongly correlated electron pairs, with a new cutoff scaling of up to ~5.5 Uₚ versus the usual ~3.2 Uₚ; ellipticity dependence and TDSE support a coherent rescattering origin rather than plasma emission.
- **Why it matters:** High-harmonic generation has long been framed as a single-electron upconversion process; a correlation-driven soft-X-ray plateau both enlarges tabletop X-ray reach and turns HHG into a spectroscopic probe of attosecond–femtosecond electron correlations.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41566-026-01976-2

### 2. Perfect transmission through duality defects resolves a monopole paradox
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Ueda, Vander Linden, De Vos, Lootens, Haegeman, Fendley & Verstraete (Nature Physics, VoR 7 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03390-5) show that wavepackets crossing topological interfaces in quantum spin systems with non-invertible symmetries — and across duality defects that couple dual theories — transmit perfectly and convert into non-local string-like excitations. Realizing a Kramers–Wannier duality defect in a spin chain makes the Callan–Rubakov “missing outgoing state” concrete: the particle reappears in a twisted sector as a Nebelstreif (fog-wisp) string excitation, with the defect Hilbert space identified with the virtual bond dimension of the matrix-product operator for the defect line.
- **Why it matters:** The 1980s monopole-scattering paradox looked like a conservation-law violation; this work turns the modern twisted-sector resolution into a simulable condensed-matter movie and a precise characterization of topological interfaces.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-026-03390-5

### 3. Competing charge-density waves recover by different mechanisms
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Su, Lv, Zong, Müller, Chattopadhyay, Dolgirev, Singh, Straquadine et al. (Nature Physics, VoR 7 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03382-5) use time- and angle-resolved photoemission plus time-dependent Ginzburg–Landau theory on ErTe₃, which hosts two competing charge-density-wave (CDW) orders. After light excitation, the dominant CDW reforms smoothly and uniformly (second-order-like), while the long-debated subdominant CDW reappears via nucleation and growth of isolated pockets (first-order-like), providing a time-domain fingerprint of distinct transition mechanisms.
- **Why it matters:** Phase competition underlies superconductivity and other quantum orders; identifying different recovery pathways for coexisting CDWs supplies a practical ultrafast tool for sorting which interactions drive which transitions.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-026-03382-5

### 4. Equal-time correlators are simpler than wave functions
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Arkani-Hamed, Glew & Vazão (Physical Review Letters, VoR 7 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/sgm7-181s) show that recently noted simplicity of equal-time correlators follows because correlators are full spacetime integrals, whereas wave functions are half-spacetime integrals. Consequences made manifest include absence of some wave-function singularities, simpler factorization, a vanishing first subleading Laurent coefficient around every pole, and a differential operator that generates the near–total-energy-pole expansion through second order from scattering amplitudes.
- **Why it matters:** Cosmological and quantum-field correlators are what experiments and observations actually constrain; clarifying why they are structurally simpler than wave functions sharpens the analytic toolkit for amplitudes and early-universe physics.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1103/sgm7-181s

### 5. Decategorifying knot invariants via noncrossing combinatorics
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Defant & Williams (arXiv:2608.06225, 6 Aug) introduce a technique linking dual braid-group generators, Hecke images of pure braids, and factorization problems in reflection groups to knot invariants. They prove that the (a, z=0)-HOMFLYPT polynomial arises as such a factorization solution; as applications they give a new proof of EL-shellability of the noncrossing partition lattice from the Hecke image of the full twist (with a reciprocal reading of its inverse for homotopy type), and the first reflection subword models for rational noncrossing Catalan objects in crystallographic type.
- **Why it matters:** Much of modern knot theory strengthens invariants by categorification; this reverse “decategorification” supplies concrete combinatorial models that connect HOMFLYPT evaluation to Coxeter–Catalan geometry (preprint).
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06225
