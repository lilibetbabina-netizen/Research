Diamond melting under shock compression at 1 TPa

What changed
Millot, Coppari, Lazicki, Kim, Landen, Smalyuk, Celliers, Eggert and collaborators (Nature Physics, VoR 13 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03413-1) shock-compress microcrystalline diamond with combined optical velocimetry, pyrometry and X-ray diffraction. They show the diamond structure persists up to 1 TPa — contradicting a prior claim of a transition to the BC8 phase that DFT predicts as thermodynamically stable near that pressure — and find evidence for shock-induced melting with melting temperature near ~7,300 K that decreases slightly with increasing pressure, bringing experiment into line with quantum simulations.
Why it matters
Carbon’s terapascal phase diagram anchors models of ice-giant interiors and high-density-carbon ablators in inertial confinement fusion; resolving the melt curve and ruling out an early BC8 transition under these shock paths tightens both planetary and fusion design constraints.
Key source
Nature Physics — Millot et al. (DOI 10.1038/s41567-026-03413-1)

Mott gap at the Dirac point in slow moiré Dirac fermions

What changed
Yang, Liang, Hu, Kaushal, Hsu, Watanabe, Taniguchi, Dadap, Li, Franz, Ye and co-authors (Nature Communications, VoR 14 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76783-y) realize a honeycomb moiré superlattice in twisted MoSe2 homobilayers whose graphene-like bands have Fermi velocity reduced by nearly two orders of magnitude relative to graphene. These slow Γ-valley Dirac bands (negligible spin–orbit coupling, full SU(2) symmetry) enter the strongly correlated regime; correlating Rydberg-exciton sensing with moiré exciton-polarons, they detect a Mott gap at the Dirac point that persists up to 110 K, plus correlated states at ν = −1 with weak ferromagnetic coupling and at fractional fillings.
Why it matters
A long-sought Dirac-point Mott insulator — elusive in pristine graphene because interactions are weak relative to the Fermi velocity — becomes accessible in a tunable TMD moiré platform, opening a route to many-body Dirac physics with SU(2) symmetry.
Key source
Nature Communications — Yang et al. (DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76783-y)

Interaction-limited conductivity in twisted bilayer graphene via giant THz photoresistance

What changed
Shilov, Elesin, Kravtsov, Covey, Ganichev, Kashchenko and collaborators (Nature Communications, VoR 13 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76554-9) use terahertz excitation to heat electrons while keeping the lattice cold, separating electron–electron from electron–phonon contributions to resistivity in twisted bilayer graphene. They observe giant THz photoresistance (several kΩ), showing that electron–electron scattering remains significant even in regimes previously attributed to phonons — including linear-in-T resistivity near the magic angle — and identify breakdown of Galilean invariance in the Dirac dispersion (enabling inter-valley electron–electron collisions) as a possible origin.
Why it matters
Competing stories of phonon-limited transport, strange metallicity and heavy-fermion renormalization in magic-angle TBG are directly tested; hot-electron THz transport becomes a practical tool for disentangling scattering channels in correlated 2D metals.
Key source
Nature Communications — Shilov et al. (DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76554-9)

Completing Banach’s isometric conjecture in the real case

What changed
Lu & Yang (arXiv:2608.13536, 13 Aug) prove Banach’s 1932 isometric conjecture for every odd dimension n: a real Banach space whose n-dimensional subspaces are all isometric must be a Hilbert space. Combined with Gromov’s even-dimensional result, this completes the conjecture over the reals. The argument mixes bundle topology with Brouwer degree theory. (An earlier Dec 2025 preprint by Ning Zhang, arXiv:2512.04628, also claims a complete finite-dimensional solution via John ellipsoids; the two approaches should be compared by the community.)
Why it matters
Banach’s question is a foundational rigidity problem in functional analysis and convex geometry; a claimed completion for all odd n would close the last classical cases over ℝ (preprint).
Key source
arXiv:2608.13536

Affirmative answer to Gromov’s volume growth conjecture

What changed
Ge (arXiv:2608.13553, 13 Aug) proves Gromov’s 1986 conjecture: every complete noncompact n-dimensional Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature and scalar curvature at least one satisfies Volg(B(p,R)) ≤ Cn Rn−2 for all p and R > 0. The proof uses the heat-kernel Fisher metric and Nash entropy.
Why it matters
The conjecture links lower scalar-curvature bounds to polynomial volume growth; an affirmative proof sharpens geometric analysis under Ricci ≥ 0 (preprint).
Key source
arXiv:2608.13553
