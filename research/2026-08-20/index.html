Vacuum fluctuations experimentally enhance superconductivity in NbSe₂

What changed
Wang, Cardoso, Yang and collaborators embedded layered NbSe2 in a split-ring cavity resonator and observed a higher superconducting critical temperature. Near the transition, critical current and field also rose sharply. Their calculations are consistent with cavity-mode hybridization lowering the superconducting state's energy.
Why it matters
The peer-reviewed experiment demonstrates non-invasive control of a collective material phase through electromagnetic vacuum fluctuations, with potential relevance to superconducting and quantum devices.

Nature · 19 Aug 2026

Atomic columns become a double-slit probe of bond vibrations

What changed
Tabata, Seki, Susi and collaborators focused an electron probe across two silicon [110] columns 1.36 Å apart. Double-slit-like fringes persisted from 300 K to 900 K because correlated atomic motion preserved coherence; comparison with phonon simulations yielded direction-dependent vibrational correlations linked to local bond stiffness.
Why it matters
The method offers a route to bond-resolved lattice dynamics at defects and interfaces, where local vibrations govern heat transport and material function.
Source
Nature · 19 Aug 2026

S301 offers a stellar route to measuring Sagittarius A*'s spin

What changed
Abd El Dayem, Abuter, Aimar and collaborators report a main-sequence star on an 8.7-year, highly eccentric orbit. S301 approaches within 136–142 Schwarzschild radii and reaches about 25,000 km s−1. Forecasts suggest that future astrometry and spectroscopy could detect spin-induced frame dragging within roughly a decade for favourable configurations.
Why it matters
A stellar-orbit spin measurement would directly test frame dragging and the Kerr description of a massive black hole. This is not yet a spin detection: radial velocity and stellar-perturbation modelling remain necessary.
Source
Nature · 19 Aug 2026

A Hodge-signature formula is extended to every convex polytope

What changed
Jacob B. Wood uses combinatorial intersection cohomology to extend the Maxim-Schuermann signature formula from rational polytopes to arbitrary convex polytopes, and discusses a combinatorial Hodge index theorem for polytopes.
Why it matters
The preprint carries a geometric invariant beyond rational-coordinate shapes, strengthening the bridge between algebraic and combinatorial convex geometry.
Source
arXiv:2608.18003 · 18 Aug 2026
