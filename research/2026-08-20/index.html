Conformal field theory spectra observed in a Rydberg quantum simulator

What changed
Sun, Le, Naus, Tsai, Picard, Murciano, Knap, Alicea, Endres and collaborators (Nature, VoR 19 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10904-x) directly resolve finite-size energy excitation spectra of emergent conformal field theories at quantum phase transitions in a Rydberg-atom chain. Using many-body modulation spectroscopy on chains of up to ~35 atoms, they recover universal energy ratios of the Ising and tricritical Ising CFTs, sort excitations by reflection parity with local end control, and map a dynamical structure factor tied to an underlying Ising conformal correlator.
Why it matters
CFT spectra have long governed critical phenomena on paper; measuring the predicted “energy ladder” in a programmable quantum simulator turns universality class identification into an experimental spectroscopy problem.
Source
doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10904-x

Vacuum fluctuations enhance superconductivity in cavity-embedded NbSe₂

What changed
Wang, Cardoso, Yang, Gong, Zhang, Zhu, Zhang, Pan, Cai, Chen, Jiang, Cheng and collaborators (Nature, VoR 19 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-11037-x) embed superconducting NbSe2 in a split-ring cavity resonator and report an increase in critical temperature, with critical current and critical field rising dramatically near the transition. They interpret the enhancement as hybridisation between electronic degrees of freedom and fluctuating cavity vacuum modes that lowers the superconducting-state energy.
Why it matters
Noninvasive, cavity-mediated control of Tc would add a new knob for quantum materials without chemical doping or continuous drive fields.
Source
doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11037-x

Atomic-scale double-slit interferometry inside a crystal

What changed
Tabata, Seki, Susi, Ishikawa & Shibata (Nature, VoR 19 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10914-9) use a focused STEM electron probe delocalised over two adjacent Si [110] columns 1.36 Å apart to generate Young-type interference fringes. Fringes persist from 300 K to 900 K; quantitative visibility analysis extracts vibrational correlations between the two columns, mapping to anisotropic bond stiffness and low-energy lattice dynamics at a single pair of atomic “slits.”
Why it matters
Extending double-slit interferometry to ångström slit separations gives local, quantitative access to correlated atomic motion that conventional diffraction averages away.
Source
doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10914-9

Star S301 is sensitive to the spin of Sagittarius A*

What changed
Abd El Dayem, Abuter, Aimar, Amaro-Seoane and the GRAVITY/Sgr A* collaboration (Nature, VoR 19 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10894-w) report a faint main-sequence star S301 (mK = 19.3) on an 8.7-year, highly eccentric orbit that reaches ~25,000 km s−1 at pericentre. Within current near-infrared interferometry and future extremely large telescope spectroscopy, S301’s motion is directly sensitive to Kerr (order β3) corrections from the spin of the 4.3×106 M⊙ Galactic-centre black hole; its eccentricity suggests capture via the Hills mechanism.
Why it matters
Stellar orbits have already tested Schwarzschild (β2) effects around Sgr A*; a star sensitive to spin opens a concrete path to measuring the Kerr parameter of the nearest massive black hole.
Source
doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10894-w

A path for JUNO to detect the solar MSW transition

What changed
Nairat, Beacom, Kelly & Li (Physical Review Letters, VoR 19 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/qhr8-qhhp) propose a technique to suppress muon-induced spallation backgrounds and show that JUNO could measure the energy-dependent Mikheyev–Smirnov–Wolfenstein transition in the solar electron-neutrino survival probability at >4σ in 10 years — a central prediction of neutrino mixing that has not yet been conclusively observed as an energy-dependent solar feature.
Why it matters
A clean MSW detection would cement the matter-effect half of the three-flavor framework and strengthen the science case for next-generation neutrino detectors.
Source
doi.org/10.1103/qhr8-qhhp

Holographic a-maximization survives higher-derivative AdS₅ gravity

What changed
Hristov, Khandelwal, Pang & Tartaglino-Mazzucchelli (Physical Review Letters, VoR 18 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1103/nrlq-ktd1) develop a partially off-shell framework for higher-derivative five-dimensional N=1 gauged supergravity on AdS5 and show that the holographic on-shell action still reproduces the trial a-anomaly of the dual CFT4, so a-maximization continues to select the correct superconformal R-symmetry beyond two-derivative gravity.
Why it matters
AdS/CFT checks often stop at Einstein gravity; surviving a-maximization under higher-derivative corrections strengthens the holographic dictionary in a more realistic bulk.
Source
doi.org/10.1103/nrlq-ktd1

Near-optimal linear range for the Erdős matching conjecture

What changed
Cao, Liu & Zhang (arXiv:2608.19118, 19 Aug) prove that for every fixed k≥2 there is s0(k) such that whenever s≥s0(k) and n≥(k+1)s, every k-uniform family on [n] with matching number ≤s has size at most binom(n,k)−binom(n−s,k), with equality only for all k-sets meeting a fixed s-set. This lowers the best previous general linear coefficient for n/s from (5k−2)/3 to k+1, approaching the conjectured threshold where the “hitting” construction dominates.
Why it matters
The Erdős matching conjecture is a central extremal-set-theory dichotomy; pushing the proven linear window closer to the conjectured transition sharpens where the two extremal constructions compete (preprint).
Source
arxiv.org/abs/2608.19118
