## Findings

### 1. Vacuum fluctuations experimentally enhance superconductivity in NbSe₂
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Wang, Cardoso, Yang and collaborators (Nature, published 19 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-11037-x) embed layered NbSe₂ in a split-ring cavity resonator and observe a higher superconducting critical temperature. Near the transition, the critical current and critical field also rise sharply. Their calculations are consistent with hybridization between the material's electronic degrees of freedom and fluctuating cavity modes lowering the superconducting state's energy.
- **Why it matters:** This is proof-of-principle evidence that electromagnetic vacuum fluctuations can tune a collective material phase without chemical substitution or direct optical driving, opening a non-invasive control route for superconducting and quantum devices.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11037-x

### 2. Atomic columns become a double-slit probe of bond vibrations
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Tabata, Seki, Susi and collaborators (Nature, published 19 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10914-9) use a focused scanning-transmission-electron-microscope probe across two neighbouring silicon [110] atomic columns, 1.36 Å apart, to generate double-slit-like interference. Fringes persist from 300 K to 900 K because correlated atomic motion preserves coherence. Comparing the patterns with phonon simulations lets the team extract direction-dependent vibrational correlations and relate them to local bond stiffness.
- **Why it matters:** Established phonon measurements average over large volumes or momentum space. Turning a selected atomic pair into an interferometer creates a route to bond-resolved lattice dynamics at defects and interfaces, where local vibrations control heat transport and material performance.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10914-9

### 3. S301 offers a stellar route to measuring Sagittarius A*'s spin
- **Field:** physics
- **What changed:** Abd El Dayem, Abuter, Aimar and collaborators (Nature, published 19 Aug 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10894-w) report S301, a faint main-sequence star on an 8.7-year, highly eccentric orbit around the Milky Way's central black hole. Its inferred pericentre is only 136–142 Schwarzschild radii and its peak speed is about 25,000 km s⁻¹. Forecasts indicate that continued interferometric astrometry plus future extremely-large-telescope spectroscopy could detect spin-induced Lense–Thirring precession within roughly a decade, depending on spin magnitude and orientation.
- **Why it matters:** A dynamical spin measurement from a stellar orbit would test frame dragging and the Kerr description of a massive black hole through a comparatively direct gravitational probe. The forecast is not yet a spin detection: S301 currently lacks a measured radial velocity and stellar perturbations must be modelled.
- **Confidence:** high
- **Key source:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10894-w

### 4. A Hodge-signature formula is extended to every convex polytope
- **Field:** maths
- **What changed:** Jacob B. Wood (arXiv:2608.18003, submitted 18 Aug 2026) uses combinatorial intersection cohomology to extend the Maxim-Schuermann signature formula from rational polytopes, which correspond to projective toric varieties, to arbitrary convex polytopes. The preprint also formulates a combinatorial version of the Hodge index theorem for polytopes.
- **Why it matters:** The result transfers a geometric invariant beyond polytopes that can be encoded by rational coordinates, strengthening the bridge between algebraic geometry and purely combinatorial convex geometry. It is a new preprint and has not yet passed journal peer review.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Key source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18003
